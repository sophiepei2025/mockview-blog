## U of T Engineering — Admissions Process

All applicants apply through OUAC — the 101 application for current Ontario high school students, the 105 application for others. After submitting, U of T sends setup instructions for your JOIN U of T account, which gives you access to the **Engineering Applicant Portal** to complete your **Online Student Profile (OSP)** — a mandatory part of the application.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth knowing:</strong> OUAC and OSP deadlines, including any early-consideration window, shift somewhat year to year. Submitting early doesn't improve your odds of admission — it only means you might hear back sooner. Confirm current dates on <a href="https://discover.engineering.utoronto.ca/how-to-apply/">U of T Engineering's how-to-apply page</a> rather than a date quoted here.
</div>

As part of your application, you'll choose between two pathways:

- **Core 8, Declared Engineering** — Chemical, Civil, Electrical & Computer, Industrial, Materials, Mechanical, or Mineral Engineering, if you already know your focus
- **Engineering Science (EngSci)** — a direct-entry program with a shared, more theory-heavy foundation in Years 1–2, followed by choosing 1 of 8 majors for Years 3–4

If you're not sure, **TrackOne** lets you explore several areas in first year before declaring a Core 8 program at year's end.

### Admission Requirements & Average

Requirements vary depending on whether you're an Ontario 101 applicant, a non-Ontario 105 applicant, or applying from outside Canada — check the requirements page relevant to your situation rather than a generic list, since specifics vary by province and country.

U of T Engineering uses your top six Grade 12 U/M courses (including prerequisites) for your admission average. There's a stated minimum course average below which applications aren't considered — but that minimum is well below what's actually competitive; historically, competitive averages for incoming students have run considerably higher. If your average sits below the competitive range, the Online Student Profile becomes where you make up ground — U of T Engineering evaluates holistically, weighing skills, extracurriculars, and how you communicate alongside your grades.

### Acceptance Rate & Timeline

U of T Engineering receives a large volume of applications every year — among the highest of any Canadian engineering program. Because the university sends out more offers than it has seats (expecting some admitted students to decline), the effective acceptance rate is meaningfully lower than the raw "offers ÷ applicants" number would suggest. Both figures shift year to year, so rather than quote specific numbers here, U of T Engineering's own internal reports (published periodically) are the most reliable source if you want current data.

Decisions typically go out in a first wave in the late-winter/spring window, with a further round of offers afterward as some admitted students decline their spot — so not hearing back immediately isn't a bad sign.

## Online Student Profile & Personal Profile

The OSP has six sections: **Engineering Program Selection** (ranking up to four programs you'd consider — you'll receive one offer, so rank genuinely, not strategically), **Academic Timeline**, **Other Information** (test scores, an optional applicant statement, and a special consideration section for accessibility or personal circumstances), **Academic Profile**, **Extracurricular Involvement**, an **Extracurricular Essay** (roughly 250 words on how your activities outside class shaped your interest in the program), and the **Personal Profile** itself.

<div class="institution-callout">
  Don't under-invest in the other five sections just because the Personal Profile gets the most attention — the extracurricular and academic-profile sections are where the "broad-based admissions" evaluation actually starts.
</div>

The Personal Profile takes about 30 minutes and consists of three randomly selected questions: one written (short prep window, roughly 250-word response) and two video questions (short prep, timed response). Reviewers — trained members of the U of T community — are using a **broad-based admissions** approach, looking beyond grades at how you communicate, handle pressure, and think through problems.

Questions generally fall into two types: **personal questions** (interests, goals, teamwork, handling conflict) and **problem questions** (logical/creative reasoning tasks). Both formats can show up as either written or video prompts.

**A word on originality:** treat any example question or answer you see in a guide as a reference for structure, never something to adapt closely — plagiarized responses are usually detectable and can mean disqualification.

### Prep Tips Worth Taking Seriously

- **Practice on camera before it counts.** Seeing yourself on screen is its own adjustment, separate from knowing the content of your answer.
- **Answer the whole question.** Break multi-part prompts into pieces and address each one — it's easy to drift off-topic under time pressure.
- **Don't race the clock.** A slightly shorter, clearly paced answer reads better than a rushed one crammed with everything you know.
- **Make every answer say something about you**, even on problem-solving questions that aren't explicitly personal.
- **Be concise** — word and time limits are tighter than they feel, so cut anything repetitive before it dilutes your best point.
- **Look and sound like you take it seriously** — quiet space, clean background, eye contact with the camera — while still letting your actual personality come through.
- **Say something only you would say.** With thousands of applicants answering the same prompts, generic answers blend together; specific, real experiences don't.

## About the U of T Engineering Program

### Entry Options & Focus Areas

Each Core 8 program has its own set of focus areas within it (for example, Electrical & Computer Engineering spans areas like software, photonics, and communications/signal processing; Mechanical Engineering spans mechatronics, bioengineering, and more). EngSci's eight majors span areas from Aerospace and Robotics to Machine Intelligence and Engineering Physics, chosen after a shared Years 1–2 foundation. The current full list of focus areas and majors is best checked on U of T Engineering's program pages, since offerings are periodically updated.

### Practical Experience Requirement & Co-op

All students (Core 8 and EngSci) must complete a **Practical Experience Requirement** — a minimum number of hours of practical work experience, completed any time before graduation through paid/unpaid work, research, or industry positions. Doing a **PEY Co-op** or the **Engineering Summer Internship Program (ESIP)** automatically satisfies this requirement.

**PEY Co-op** is a long-form paid work placement (roughly 12–16 months), open to students who've completed Year 3, and is among the largest programs of its kind in Canada — giving students real exposure to applying, interviewing, and competing for engineering roles at major employers. Students indicate intent to pursue PEY when they apply, and U of T Engineering runs preparatory programming in Years 1 and 2 (career exploration, skill-building modules) so students are ready to compete for placements by the end of Year 3.

**ESIP** is a shorter, four-month paid summer work term, now integrated into the same PEY preparation pipeline as an option after Year 2.

### Courses

Each Core 8 program has its own required and elective course sequence; EngSci shares a common Years 1–2 curriculum before splitting into major-specific courses in Years 3–4. Full, current course lists are best checked on U of T Engineering's academic calendar, since course offerings are updated fairly regularly.

### Tuition & Scholarships

Tuition varies by program and domestic/international status and changes yearly — check U of T's official tuition page for current figures. U of T Engineering offers a range of entrance scholarships and awards; eligibility and amounts are updated each cycle, so its scholarships page is the reliable source rather than a number quoted in a guide.

### Student Life

Toronto's downtown campus puts students close to a major city's worth of internships, events, and extracurricular options, alongside a large number of engineering-specific clubs, design teams, and competitions to get involved in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How hard is it to get into U of T Engineering?**
It's one of the more competitive engineering programs in Canada — strong grades get you into consideration, but the holistic, broad-based evaluation means the Online Student Profile carries real weight too.

**What's U of T Engineering known for?**
A large, well-resourced program with strong industry and research connections in downtown Toronto, plus one of the largest co-op-style work placement programs (PEY) of its kind in the country.

**What's first year like?**
Core 8 students take a largely common first-year course load within their program; EngSci students share a common foundation across all EngSci students regardless of eventual major — both are demanding, foundational years before more specialization kicks in.
