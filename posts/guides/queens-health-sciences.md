# Queen's Health Sciences: The Definitive Guide for Applicants

*by Edmoat*

If you're applying to Queen's University for the **Bachelor of Health Sciences (BHSc)** — or just trying to understand whether it's the right fit — this guide breaks down the admissions process, the supplementary application, program structure, and the questions students ask most.

This guide focuses primarily on the on-campus Queen's BHSc program; the online BHSc has a separate application process worth checking directly on Queen's site if that's the track you're considering.

## Queen's Health Sciences — Admission Process

The application has two components:

1. **Online Application** — submitted through OUAC (101 for current Ontario high school students, 105 for others). Queen's Kingston on-campus has its own program code; the UK campus (BISC) and online program use separate codes — confirm the current codes on Queen's own admissions page, since these occasionally change.
2. **Supplementary Application** — one timed written response and one timed video response, completed on a third-party platform after your OUAC application is in.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth knowing:</strong> Application windows and deadlines for both the OUAC and Supplementary components shift somewhat year to year. Always confirm current dates on <a href="https://www.queensu.ca/admission/key-dates-and-deadlines">Queen's key dates page</a> rather than a date quoted in a guide like this one.
</div>

Academic requirements vary by curriculum (Canadian high school vs. international), so use Queen's own requirements tool for your specific situation rather than a generic course list.

### Admission Average

Queen's BHSc sets a minimum cumulative average for consideration, along with a minimum grade in ENG4U — but the competitive range in practice tends to run higher than the stated minimum. This is exactly where the **Supplementary Application** does real work: a strong Supp App can meaningfully offset an average that's solid but not exceptional.

### Acceptance Rate

Queen's BHSc is a **highly competitive program** — a large applicant pool for a relatively small number of first-year spots, consistently among the more selective undergraduate programs in Canada. The exact numbers shift from cycle to cycle, so rather than quote a specific rate here, your best source is Queen's own admissions FAQ page, updated for the current cycle.

### Acceptance Timeline

Queen's reviews applications and sends decisions on a rolling basis, which means submitting earlier — without rushing a weaker application out the door — tends to be an advantage. If you haven't heard back by mid-spring, that's not necessarily bad news; Queen's continues assessing applications later into the cycle than some other schools.

## Queen's Health Sciences — Supplementary Application

The Supplementary Application is mandatory, opening once you've submitted your OUAC application. Queen's evaluates it against published rubrics — one for the written response, one for the video response — assessing more than just grades: communication, self-awareness, and how well you can convey your own experiences and goals.

<div class="institution-callout">
  Even applicants with strong averages can end up with a weaker overall application if the Supplementary responses feel generic or rehearsed. The written and video responses are where reviewers actually get a sense of who you are — treat them accordingly.
</div>

## About the Queen's Health Sciences (BHSc) Program

The BHSc at Queen's is a four-year, direct-entry program built around how the body functions in health and disease, how it defends itself, and how medications are used to treat illness — with an emphasis on the societal and global context of health, not just the biology.

### Learning Approach: Flipped Classroom

Queen's BHSc uses a "flipped classroom" model for on-campus students: rather than lecture-then-homework, students work through online modules and self-assessments *before* class, then use class time for small-group discussion and application with a faculty member or TA — working through harder concepts using real case studies.

<div class="institution-callout">
  This model rewards independent time management. The modules are flexible (work through them whenever fits your schedule, before the deadline), but it's a real adjustment from a traditional high-school lecture format — students who struggle with self-directed pacing tend to find the first term the hardest.
</div>

### Competencies Framework

Queen's structures the program around seven competencies adapted from the CanMEDS framework used in medical education — Collaborator, Communicator, Health Advocate, Leader, Scholar, Professional, and Content Expert. Every course and assessment in the program reinforces these, which gives graduates a head start if they go on to medical school or another health profession — they're not learning these skills from scratch once they get there.

### Learning Tracks

Students choose an optional Learning Track at the end of Year 1, allowing deeper focus in a specific area — options include tracks like Anatomical Basis of Health and Disease, Applied Research Methods, Global and Population Health, Infection/Immunity/Inflammation, Molecular Basis of Biology, Pharmacology/Toxicology/Therapeutics, and Physiological Basis of Health and Disease. Completing a track's required courses gets it noted on your transcript.

### Courses

Each year has a mix of core, option, and elective courses, with upper-year students able to take a limited number of Personal Interest Credits (pass/fail, outside the program) for a more interdisciplinary experience. First-year core courses cover areas like human anatomy, statistics for health sciences, pharmacology, and the history and philosophy of health and healthcare — including hands-on access to Queen's Anatomy Learning Centre. The full current course list and requirements are best checked directly on Queen's program page, since course codes and offerings do get updated.

### QuARMS

The Queen's Accelerated Route to Medical School (QuARMS) lets eligible students apply to Queen's Medical School after two years of the BHSc, without the MCAT — a distinctive pathway not offered elsewhere in Canada in quite the same form. Eligibility criteria are specific, so check the current requirements directly on Queen's site.

### Study Abroad

First-year BHSc students have the option to study at the Bader International Study Centre (BISC) at Herstmonceux Castle in England before returning to Kingston for Year 2 — a distinctive feature of the program most applicants don't expect.

### Internships

BHSc students (on-campus and online) are eligible for paid work terms through the Queen's Undergraduate Internship Program (QUIP), typically in the 2nd or 3rd year, with a "Professional Internship" designation added to the transcript on completion.

### Tuition & Scholarships

Tuition and fees vary by domestic/international status and change from year to year — check Queen's official tuition page for current numbers rather than a figure quoted here. Queen's offers a range of admission scholarships, awards, and bursaries; some you're automatically considered for when you apply, others require a separate application — worth checking Queen's financial aid pages early in your planning.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are classes like?**
Smaller than a typical lecture hall (often 20 students or fewer), built around discussion rather than passive note-taking, since the content itself is already covered via the flipped-classroom modules before class.

**How does Queen's Health Sci prepare students for med school?**
Primarily through the Competencies Framework — students graduate already having practiced the collaboration, communication, and leadership skills medical schools look for, rather than starting from zero.

**What's the difference between Health Sciences and Life Sciences?**
Health Sciences focuses specifically on human health and disease with a strong societal/global lens and more room for electives outside the program; Life Sciences covers all living things with a stronger biomedical science focus. Both offer research and experiential learning opportunities.

## How Queen's BHSc Compares to Similar Programs

Queen's BHSc is often compared to programs like McMaster's Health Sciences (BHSc) — both are highly ranked, highly competitive, and built around a non-traditional teaching model (Queen's flipped classroom vs. McMaster's inquiry-based learning). Rather than compare specific acceptance rates or tuition figures here — both shift yearly — the more useful distinction is pedagogical: if you'd rather learn material independently before applying it in class, Queen's flipped model may suit you; if you prefer working from open-ended questions toward an answer, an inquiry-based model might fit better.
