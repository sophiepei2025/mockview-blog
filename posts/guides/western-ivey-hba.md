# Western Ivey HBA: Supplementary Application Guide, Examples & Templates

*by Edmoat*

If you're looking for guidance on the **Western Ivey HBA supplementary application** — what it includes, how it's evaluated, and how to actually write it — this guide walks through the whole thing.

Ivey's admissions committee reads thousands of applications a year. What tends to separate the memorable ones isn't a clever gimmick — it's a clear sense of who the applicant is, told through specific, well-chosen experiences and communicated cleanly. That's harder than it sounds, and it's what most of this guide is about.

Note that this guide won't answer the questions for you — the goal is to help you understand how to express your own ideas more effectively. Your responses need to be your own words and your own experiences.

## Western Ivey Supplementary Application — Overview

Western Ivey HBA AEO asks applicants to complete a supplementary application alongside their OUAC submission. The main components are:

| # | Section | Format | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Awards | List | — |
| 2 | Activity 1 | Written essay | 500 words |
| 3 | Activity 2 | Written essay | 500 words |
| 4 | Additional Activities | List | — |
| 5–9 | Video Questions (x5) | Live video, 60s prep | 1m30s response |

There's also an optional **Identity Supplemental** essay (roughly 500 words) about identity and belonging — it's optional, but worth genuine thought rather than skipping by default.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth remembering:</strong> Ivey AEO is a status, not a standalone program — you apply to another program at Western (Engineering, Arts & Humanities, Social Science, etc.) and separately indicate your intention to apply for Ivey AEO status on your OUAC application. Exact requirements, essay prompts, and the application deadline can shift from cycle to cycle, so always confirm the current details directly on <a href="https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/hba/admissions/">Ivey's admissions page</a> rather than a past cycle's specifics.
</div>

The activities section asks you to describe each activity individually in a way that demonstrates qualities like leadership, teamwork, initiative, achievement, commitment, integrity, resilience, and breadth of involvement — and to use your full word count rather than leaving it thin. Ivey caps the total number of activities you can list, split between the two required written essays and an additional list section for extracurriculars beyond those two.

For every activity you mention, you'll need to provide a verifiable reference — name, role, phone number, and email. Applications with unverifiable references won't be considered, so this isn't a section to treat casually.

## Western Ivey Application: Awards

Before the essays, there's an optional Awards section where you can list non-academic awards, scholarships, or distinctions from recent years, with a short description for each.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Tip:</strong> Context matters more than the award title alone. An award selected by a jury from a large pool reads differently than one automatically given to every participant — where relevant, briefly note how competitive or selective the award was.
</div>

**Example format:**

> **Award Title:** Edmonton Journal All-Star
> **Granting Institution:** Edmonton Journal
> **Description:** Selected as one of a small group of top athletes in the region.
> **Date:** March 2021

If you don't feel like you have anything that fits neatly under "Awards," don't worry — plenty of strong applicants leave this section light and make up for it in the Activity Essays, which carry far more weight.

## Western Ivey Application: Activity Essays

You'll be asked to describe your top two activities from recent years — community involvement, paid or unpaid work, caregiving, creative projects, entrepreneurship, or other meaningful commitments — each in its own 500-word essay, written in paragraph form.

Think of these essays the way you'd think about meeting someone for the first time: the admissions committee knows nothing about you going in, and it's through the specifics you share — what you did, the role you took on, what changed as a result — that they start to actually get a sense of who you are. This is your one shot at that first impression, so the two activities you pick matter as much as how you write about them.

Ivey is looking for evidence of leadership, teamwork, initiative, and genuine achievement — so when you're choosing which two activities to write about, lean toward the ones that show real ownership and impact, not just participation.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Tip:</strong> Strong activity essays tend to share a similar shape, even when the content is completely different — they set up the context briefly (when, why, what your role was), describe a real moment of initiative or change you drove, show the actual outcome or impact on others, and close with one specific thing you learned and how it's shaped you since. You don't need to hit every beat mechanically, but essays that skip straight from "I joined the club" to "it taught me a lot" without any specific moment in between tend to blur together for reviewers who are reading hundreds of these.
</div>

Go past the surface-level account of what you did — the essays that stand out usually show that the experience actually changed how the applicant thinks or acts, not just that they showed up consistently.

**A word on originality:** using an examples-based guide like this one to understand structure is fine; copying language or specifics from an example essay is plagiarism, is usually detectable, and can disqualify an application outright. Treat any example as a reference for shape, never as a draft.

## Western Ivey Application: Additional Activities

If you have other extracurriculars you don't want to write a full essay about but still want on record, there's a separate list-format section for them — bullet points rather than essay form, covering activities from the same eligibility window as the two written essays.

This section is optional, but the same reference-verification requirement applies: anything you list here still needs a verifiable contact who can confirm it.

## Western Ivey Application: References

For every activity listed anywhere in your application, you'll need to provide a reference — name, role, and contact information. After you submit, Ivey contacts each reference directly to confirm the details you provided (role, dates, hours committed, description) are accurate; the reference simply confirms or disputes what you wrote.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Tip:</strong> Don't inflate your role, hours, or impact — the fastest way to damage an application is a reference who doesn't recognize the description they're being asked to confirm. Give your references a heads-up before you submit, and briefly walk them through what you wrote so there are no surprises when Ivey reaches out.
</div>
