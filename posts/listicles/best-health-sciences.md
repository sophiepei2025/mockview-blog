# Best Health Sciences Programs in Canada (Undergraduate)

*by Edmoat*

Health Sciences programs in Canada vary more than they get credit for — some are essentially structured pre-med pipelines, others are deliberately broad and interdisciplinary, and the teaching model itself (lecture vs. flipped classroom vs. inquiry-based) can shape your day-to-day experience as much as the subject matter does. Here's a look at five well-regarded undergraduate Health Sciences (BHSc) programs, organized around what genuinely differentiates each.

## McMaster Health Sciences BHSc (Hamilton, Ontario)

McMaster's BHSc has a strong reputation as a pre-med pathway — a meaningful share of graduates go on to medical school, and McMaster's own med school draws heavily from its BHSc alumni, which makes for a smoother transition for students who stay at the same institution.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  McMaster's defining feature is its inquiry-based, student-driven learning model — small classes (around 20 students) working through problems and research together rather than sitting through large lectures. It's the only Ontario program drawing on the full range of health sciences departments in this way.
</blockquote>

Even for students not set on medicine, the program's flexibility (electives, certificates in other disciplines) keeps other career paths genuinely open, not just medicine-adjacent ones.

## Queen's Health Sciences BHSc (Kingston, Ontario)

Queen's BHSc uses a "flipped classroom" model — online modules and self-assessments before class, small-group discussion and application during it — a structural break from lecture-based learning that some students find dramatically more engaging, and others find requires more self-discipline than they expected.

<div class="program-badge">Competencies Framework</div> — Queen's structures the curriculum around seven core competencies (Collaborator, Communicator, Health Advocate, Leader, Scholar, Professional, Content Expert) adapted from medical education, giving graduates a practical head start if they go on to a health profession afterward.

Queen's is also the only Canadian program offering a fully online BHSc degree alongside the on-campus option — a genuinely distinctive flexibility for students who need to study on their own schedule or aren't based in Kingston.

## Western Health Sciences (London, Ontario)

Western doesn't require students to pick a specialization ("module") until the end of first year, giving genuine room to explore before committing — paired with real freedom to pull in coursework from philosophy, psychology, anthropology, and other fields alongside the health sciences core.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  Western requires several community-service or international-experience courses as part of the core curriculum — including a distinctive Global Skills Opportunity based in Scandinavia — which pushes students into real-world healthcare contexts earlier than a purely classroom-based program would.
</blockquote>

Western also offers a combined degree option with Ivey Business School — two degrees (BSc and HBA) completed in five years rather than doing them separately, a strong fit if you're interested in the business side of healthcare specifically.

## Waterloo Health Sciences (Waterloo, Ontario)

Waterloo's Health Sciences program deliberately blends hard science (biology, chemistry, physiology) with the societal factors that shape health (policy, economics) — first-year coursework spans both the Faculty of Health and Faculty of Medicine, which is a genuinely different balance than programs that lean heavily one way or the other.

<div class="institution-callout">
  Waterloo's co-op — one of the largest in Canada — gives Health Sciences students close to two years of real-world experience alternating with study terms, with past placements at organizations like Cancer Care Ontario and the Centre for Addiction and Mental Health. Before placements, Waterloo's Professional Development Program (WatPD) builds career-readiness skills through mandatory and elective coursework.
</div>

## uOttawa Health Sciences (Ottawa, Ontario)

uOttawa's BHSc is built around choosing one of three interdisciplinary core areas (Population & Public Health, Technologies & Innovation, or Integrative Health Biosciences), giving students real breadth beyond a strictly biomedical focus — a good fit if you're not fully certain what direction within health sciences interests you most.

There's no co-op here, but research is genuinely central — the Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program pairs students with faculty mentors, and the program supports research thesis projects for students who want that experience before graduate school.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth knowing:</strong> uOttawa's BHSc doesn't automatically include the prerequisites for uOttawa's own med school — if medicine is the goal, you'll need to plan your electives deliberately rather than assume the degree covers it by default.
</div>
