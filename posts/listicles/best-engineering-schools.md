# Best Engineering Schools in Canada (Undergraduate Programs)

*by Edmoat*

Choosing an engineering program in Canada usually comes down to one core question: do you want to declare your specific program on day one, or spend a common first year exploring before committing? From there, co-op scale, research opportunities, and student support systems are what actually separate programs. Here's a rundown of eight of Canada's most-discussed undergraduate engineering programs, organized around what makes each genuinely different.

## University of Waterloo Engineering

Waterloo is one of the largest engineering schools in the country, and — unusually — students start their chosen program from day one of first year rather than doing a common foundational year. That means more upfront research is needed to pick the right program, but it also means more directly relevant training from the start.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  Waterloo Engineering's co-op is mandatory and among the largest in the world, giving students up to two years of real-world work experience across thousands of potential employers by the time they graduate — plus the practical advantage of being based in a region with a genuinely dense tech industry.
</blockquote>

## University of Toronto Engineering

Canada's oldest and largest engineering school, and one of the few offering Engineering Science (EngSci) — a shared, more theoretical foundation across math, science, computing, and humanities in Years 1–2, before choosing 1 of 8 majors. Alongside EngSci, there are seven other "Core 8" declared-engineering programs, plus a TrackOne option for students who want a common first year before deciding.

<!-- [IMAGE: illustration | 学生在工程实验室做项目的场景 | alt="Student working on an engineering project in a lab"] -->

U of T doesn't just place students into a co-op — the Practical Experience Requirement (PEY Co-op, ESIP, or equivalent) comes with structured preparation beforehand: career exploration programming and skill-building modules so students are actually ready to compete for placements, not just thrown into the job market.

## McGill University Engineering

McGill offers fewer distinct programs than Waterloo or U of T, but its Electrical and Computer Engineering track stands out — including an Enhanced Power Concentration with industry-sponsored courses and lab access through its affiliated power engineering institute, a genuinely distinctive opportunity if that's your area of interest.

McGill also leans into undergraduate research: its Summer Undergraduate Research in Engineering (SURE) program — including an international version — pairs students directly with a faculty mentor, which is particularly valuable if graduate school or a research career is the eventual goal.

## University of British Columbia Engineering

UBC Engineering starts everyone on the same first-year curriculum (chemistry, math, physics, intro engineering), then opens into one of the widest ranges of second-year concentrations among Canadian programs.

<div class="institution-callout">
  UBC's co-op is the largest in Western Canada, with a genuinely broad base of participating employers — and a real practical benefit: students frequently report the co-op experience either confirming their career direction or helping them pivot early, before graduation rather than after.
</div>

Students who want breadth beyond engineering itself can pursue a Minor or a Dual Degree in another faculty (Arts, Science, Commerce), making UBC a solid fit for students who aren't fully certain engineering-only is the right lane.

## McMaster University Engineering

McMaster's curriculum — branded internally as "The Pivot" — is built around experiential learning from day one, including a first-year design course where students complete hands-on projects (coding, materials, graphic design) and showcase them at year's end, rather than sitting through a purely lecture-based first year.

McMaster also runs the second-largest co-op program in Ontario and has one of the largest undergraduate engineering research programs in the country — a strong combination if you want both hands-on industry experience and a genuine research track available through a Summer Research Program.

## Queen's University Engineering

Queen's runs a common first year across all ten of its engineering programs, with guaranteed discipline placement from second year on — plus Dual Degree options combining a Bachelor of Applied Science with an Arts and Science degree for students who want to explore more broadly. Queen's is also the only Canadian program offering a dedicated Mechatronics and Robotics Engineering stream.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  Queen's puts real structure behind student support — an advising and mentorship network plus an online bridging program to ease the transition from high school — which shows up in how strongly current students talk about feeling supported, not just academically challenged.
</blockquote>

## University of Alberta Engineering

Alberta offers more accredited undergraduate engineering degrees than any other Canadian school, with a shared Qualifying First Year before students choose their specific program — a heavier course load upfront, but one that gives genuine exposure before committing.

Alberta's co-op is also among the largest in the country, with work terms that can add up to nearly half a degree's worth of time — a serious commitment, but one that builds a correspondingly large amount of real work experience and professional network before graduation.

## University of Calgary Engineering

Like Alberta and UBC, Calgary runs a common first year — but with two distinctive twists: guaranteed placement into your first-choice program (removing a layer of first-year anxiety), and an "engineering block" system where you take all your first-year classes with the same cohort, building community faster than a typical large first-year lecture experience would.

Calgary also lets students start building professional experience earlier than most programs — an Engineering Career Practicum after first or second year, well before the third-year-plus co-ops typical elsewhere.
