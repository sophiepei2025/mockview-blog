# Best Computer Science Universities in Canada 2026 (Undergraduate Programs)

*Updated January 2026*

Choosing a computer science program in Canada usually comes down to a handful of real trade-offs: do you want the largest co-op system in the world, or a research-heavy department that feeds straight into grad school? Do you want the freedom to combine CS with another discipline, or a program built entirely around one specialization?

There's no single "best" answer — it depends on what you're optimizing for. Below are five of Canada's strongest undergraduate CS programs, broken down by what actually makes each one distinct, not just how they rank.

## University of Toronto — Best for Flexibility Across Three Campuses

U of T Computer Science runs across three campuses — St. George (downtown Toronto), Scarborough, and Mississauga — and all three award the same Honours Bachelor of Science degree. The curriculum overlaps heavily, but each campus has its own personality:

- **UTSG (St. George)** offers the Arts & Science Internship Program (ASIP): four mandatory professional-development courses plus a 12-, 16-, or 20-month work placement, typically starting in second year.
- **UTSC (Scarborough)** is built around a co-op stream — three four-month work terms, preceded by a required Co-op Preparation course. Placements range from IBM and the major banks down to early-stage startups.
- **UTM (Mississauga)** offers a 12–16 month Professional Experience Year (PEY) with a similar apply-and-work structure.

Within any campus, you can go narrow (a Specialist in, say, Computer Systems or Game Design) or broader (a Major or Minor paired with something else). St. George requires a supplemental application on top of your OUAC submission; Scarborough and Mississauga admit on grades alone — worth knowing before you commit to which campus you're applying to.

## University of British Columbia — Best for Interdisciplinary and Dual-Degree Options

UBC's CS department is consistently ranked in Canada's top three and is one of the oldest in the country. Its defining feature is flexibility: you can pursue CS as a BSc (Faculty of Science) or a BA (Faculty of Arts), combine it with business or software engineering, or take the Second Degree Program pairing a Bachelor of Computer Science with a separate BSc.

UBC's co-op runs 8–20 months depending on the track, with placements skewed toward Vancouver's software industry — SAP, Hootsuite, and EA show up often, alongside Amazon, Microsoft, and federal employers. A department-run survey found UBC CS grads earning about 38% more than peers from comparable programs two years out — though as with any self-reported survey, treat it as a data point rather than a guarantee.

On the research side, undergrads can spend a summer working directly with faculty (16-week research awards) or join the Research Experience Program alongside grad students, with results sometimes presented at UBC's undergraduate research conference. If grad school or a research career is the eventual goal, this is one of the more accessible on-ramps at the undergrad level.

## McGill University — Best for Combining CS With Another Discipline

McGill's structure is built around combination rather than specialization. The BSc Liberal Program lets you pair 45 credits of core CS with 18 credits in another field — Arts, Management, another Science — and McGill also runs joint programs like CS+Biology, Math+CS, and CS+Physics.

Its internship structure is unusually long: the Internship Year in Science (IYS) runs 4, 8, 12, or 16 months, versus the few-week or single-term internships more common elsewhere. Students who want a lower-commitment test run can instead take the 4-month Industrial Practicum over a summer. McGill's TechFair, held twice a year, is sizable by Canadian standards — last year drew 200+ employers and 600+ students, with the majority there specifically hunting for internships rather than full-time offers.

One footnote worth knowing: Alan Emtage, who built the first internet search engine (Archie) in 1990, did it as a McGill student — a reasonable indicator of how far back the department's research culture goes.

## University of Alberta — Best for AI and Machine Learning Research

<!-- [IMAGE: data-card | Alberta CS at a glance | 卡片内容: 25+年AI/强化学习研究积累 / 依托Amii / alt="University of Alberta computing science AI research strengths"] -->

U of A's Department of Computing Science has been a recognized leader in AI research — especially reinforcement learning — for over 25 years.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute (Amii)</strong> — a research institute affiliated with U of A that's been central to the university's reputation in reinforcement learning. It's the kind of institution-level connection that doesn't show up on a program's course list but shapes what undergrads actually get exposed to.
</blockquote>

Graduates are recruited by Google, IBM, Amazon, and Imperial Oil, among others.

The program leans theoretical relative to some of the others on this list, which is a plus if grad school, research, or teaching is the eventual direction — you'll work through AI/robotics, ML/bioinformatics, and multimedia/games research tracks with direct access to faculty working in those areas. If research isn't the goal, the trade-off is that the degree is less pre-structured around a specific work-placement pipeline than Waterloo's or UBC's — the theoretical grounding still transfers well to industry roles, but you'll be building the practical experience piece yourself, through clubs like UACS, Ada's Team, or the department's programming contests.
