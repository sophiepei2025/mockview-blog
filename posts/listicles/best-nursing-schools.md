# Best Nursing Schools in Canada (Undergraduate Programs)

*by Edmoat*

Nursing programs in Canada differ less on "quality" than on structure: when clinical practice actually starts, how it's spread across the degree, and what kind of learning model (lecture, problem-based, simulation-heavy) shapes your day-to-day. Here's a look at seven well-regarded undergraduate nursing programs, organized around what genuinely sets each one apart.

## U of T Nursing (Toronto, Ontario)

U of T's Bloomberg Faculty of Nursing runs an accelerated, two-year BScN — but only after two years of prior university study (with specific prerequisites) elsewhere. That structure has a real trade-off: two years of genuine flexibility to explore other subjects first, followed by an intense, tightly focused two years once the BScN itself begins.

<blockquote class="institution-callout">
  An 11-week clinical practicum at the end of second year, chosen in collaboration with the faculty around your interests, gives access to some of Canada's leading teaching hospitals through U of T's placement network — a genuine advantage of being based in downtown Toronto.
</blockquote>

The Centre for Professional Development also offers certificate courses (leadership, acute care, wound management) and NCLEX-RN exam prep, available online or in-person, for students who want to build credentials alongside the core degree.

## University of Alberta Nursing (Edmonton, Alberta)

UAlberta Nursing stands out for sheer breadth of degree options: a Collaborative Program letting you complete your first three years at an affiliated college (Red Deer, Keyano, or Grande Prairie) before finishing at U of A; an After Degree Program for students who already hold a university degree; and a Bilingual BScN taught in both English and French for students planning to work in bilingual or Quebec settings.

<div class="program-badge">Honours BScN</div> — one of the few nursing honours programs in Canada, pairing students with a faculty mentor for genuine research work, a strong fit if graduate school, teaching, or a research-focused career is the eventual goal.

## Nursing at McMaster University (Hamilton, Ontario)

McMaster's BScN — offered jointly with Mohawk and Conestoga Colleges — uses the same problem-based learning model as McMaster Health Sciences: small groups working through limited-information scenarios rather than large lectures.

<div class="institution-callout">
  Clinical practice at McMaster starts earlier and runs longer than at many comparable programs — beginning in second year and gradually increasing through to Year 4 — rather than being concentrated into one block placement near the end. It's a different rhythm than a school like U of T, where the practicum comes as a single concentrated stretch.
</div>

McMaster also offers an Accelerated Stream (a faster path for students who've already completed two years of university) and a Post-RPN Stream for students who already hold an RPN diploma.

## Nursing at McGill University (Montreal, Quebec)

McGill's Ingram School of Nursing is built around a distinctive Strengths-Based Nursing and Healthcare approach — treating patients holistically, drawing on their own strengths and context rather than purely a clinical/diagnostic lens — woven through the curriculum and clinical training since it was implemented.

McGill is also one of the few Canadian nursing schools giving first-year students clinical exposure in the community, rather than waiting until later years — a real head start on hands-on experience compared to programs that concentrate clinical practice later in the degree.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth knowing if you plan to work in Quebec:</strong> graduates need to demonstrate French proficiency (written and verbal) to work in the province, separate from the standard licensing exam — worth planning for early if French isn't already a strength, since McGill offers coursework to help build toward it.
</div>

## Queen's University Nursing (Kingston, Ontario)

Queen's Bachelor of Nursing Science has a strong track record on licensing-exam outcomes — consistently among the highest first-attempt NCLEX pass rates in the province, which the program attributes partly to how early experiential learning starts (interviewing and assessment practice in a simulation lab from Year 1).

Clinical practice in Years 2–4 spans a genuinely wide range of settings — pediatrics, maternity, mental health, hospitalized patients, and community care — giving students real exposure across specialties before choosing a direction.

## University of Calgary Nursing (Calgary, Alberta)

UCalgary Nursing puts a heavy emphasis on hands-on practice hours — well over a thousand hours of experiential learning and clinical simulation across the degree — structured around small, consistent peer groups that work through problems together throughout the program.

<div class="institution-callout">
  UCalgary also stands out for the depth of its student-support infrastructure — dedicated mental health resources, an inclusivity committee, and peer mentorship — worth factoring in if wraparound support matters to you as much as the academics.
</div>

## Nursing at Western University (London, Ontario)

Western's nursing facilities are a real differentiator — a dedicated simulated education site with live-patient and simulator-based scenarios, plus clinical skills labs for hands-on equipment practice in a structured, lower-stakes setting before real clinical placements begin.

Western also offers real flexibility in how you get there: a Collaborative BScN with Fanshawe College (same degree, different entry points), a compressed 19-month fast-track for students with prior university credits, and an RPN-to-BScN pathway for students who already hold a Practical Nursing diploma.

<div class="institution-callout">
  <strong>Worth knowing:</strong> placement availability in obstetrics, pediatrics, and mental health specifically can be limited at Western — if one of those is your specific interest, it's worth raising directly with admissions before accepting an offer, since the school doesn't guarantee placements in those areas.
</div>
