## 1. Application Overview and What Queen's Actually Evaluates

Queen's Commerce at the Smith School of Business is one of Canada's top undergraduate business programs, with an admit rate that has held at roughly **6%**. Grade inflation means most applicants now carry averages in the 90s, including many in the mid-to-high 90s. Grades have become a status check, a baseline, and no longer separate candidates.

Queen's official assessment is explicit: **grades and the supplementary application each carry 50% of the decision.** Even a 98%+ average won't save an ordinary supplementary application.

### The scoring logic underneath: total sums scoring and dual calculation

Smith's supplementary assessment uses **total sums scoring and dual calculation**. Scores are added across each competency dimension, so a serious weakness in one dimension drags the total down in a way that can't be undone. You can't hide gaps behind one "perfect story." You need consistently strong scores across every criterion.

Queen's assessment rests on three core psychological and competency themes:

*   **Subterranean self-exploration and a reshaped mindset:** admissions is immune to a simple list of accomplishments. The assessment looks past surface achievements to **deep self-exploration**, peeling back three or four layers to show how an experience changed your underlying beliefs, your pattern of thinking (a mindset shift) and your core values.
*   **Vulnerability and self-awareness:** drop the flawless, packaged persona. Show real self-awareness: candidly analyse a decision error, a team conflict or a limit in your thinking, and explain the concrete behavioural correction you made after the setback. Maturity like this carries a lot of weight on the scoring sheet.
*   **Closing the translation gap through community and team impact:** leadership doesn't come from a title. Move from saying "I took part in these activities" (what happened) to showing "this is the real change I made" (what changed). Focus on initiative, adaptability and measurable community impact, especially when resources are limited or circumstances change suddenly.

## 2. The Supplementary Application Timeline and Steps

Most applicants who struggle underestimate how long it takes to build insight and run realistic practice. Writing essays in a rush near the January or February deadline, finding referees at the last minute or recording an improvised video without preparation, almost always leads to shallow, clichéd answers. Applicants who start in October can build real confidence and a sound strategy.

```
[October: early preparation and building the experience bank]
       │
       ▼  (build a T-shaped profile / map three or four layers of reflection)
[November–December: refining experiences and practice runs] ──► (use a Kira simulator for video and timed 10-minute, 335-word writing)
       │
       ▼  (lock in one professional source of guidance / cut outside noise)
[January: final push and steady submission] ──► (use a deliberate submission routine so you avoid last-minute risk near the deadline)
```

### Step-by-step detail

1.  **Step one: a single-source experience matrix and the T-model**
    *   Build a personal experience bank and organise it in a **T-shape**. The horizontal bar (breadth) covers two to three secondary activities. The vertical bar (depth) is one or two anchor experiences where you held real responsibility and invested over several years.
    *   For each experience, dig into initiative, adaptability, handling conflict, recovering from failure and team impact, so you always have detailed, layered material ready to draw on.
2.  **Step two: keep one consistent strategy and voice**
    *   Don't send your essays to lots of people with different views (Reddit, relatives, several teachers and friends). Outside noise pulls in different directions: parents want more emotion, teachers want academic rigour, forums question the structure. In the end your personal voice gets flattened into generic phrasing.
    *   Use one professional guidance channel, so structure, logic and tone stay consistent across the whole application.
3.  **Step three: a resilient submission plan and realistic simulation**
    *   Practise in realistic conditions, with a real camera and the 10-minute and 2-minute timers, without a script.
    *   Follow a resilient submission plan: check your equipment ahead of time (a fast, stable connection, a quiet room and a clear camera), and avoid submitting in the last 48 hours before the deadline, to rule out risks from system congestion or hardware failure.

## 3. The 10-Minute Timed Written Response (335 Words)

The written part of Queen's supplementary application uses a very strict format: a **random prompt** with an **immediate timer**.

*   **Hard limits:** 10 minutes and a 335-word maximum. There's **no separate thinking time**, and pasting is blocked in the interface.
*   **Prompt types mapped to Smith's core criteria:** prompts are drawn at random and each maps to one of Smith's core assessment areas:
    1.  *Setbacks* → **persistence and resilience**
    2.  *Uncomfortable feedback* → **reflection and self-correction**
    3.  *Unexpected change* → **adaptability**
    4.  *Resourcefulness under constraints* → **initiative and problem solving**

### Recommended structure: STAR+T with a tight word budget

To deliver a dense answer in 600 seconds, you need a strict budget of words and time under **STAR+T**:

```
[S/T: situation and task]  ──► 20% (about 65 words / 2 minutes)  ── pin down the conflict
[A: core action]           ──► 50% (about 170 words / 5 minutes) ── break down your decisions and personal choices
[R: quantified result]     ──► 15% (about 50 words / 1.5 minutes) ── show objective impact and numbers
[T: deep reflection]       ──► 15% (about 50 words / 1.5 minutes) ── deliver the reshaped thinking and behaviour change
```

*   **Situation (10%, about 30 words):** set the background quickly, without long details that don't matter.
*   **Task (10%, about 35 words):** name the core bottleneck, the risk or the specific goal you set.
*   **Action (50%, about 170 words):** **the heart of the essay.** Break down the concrete steps you took and your reasoning behind key decisions (why and how), and show what you personally contributed.
*   **Result (15%, about 50 words):** give a clear, objective outcome, and use numbers or measurable change to close the translation gap.
*   **Takeaway / deep reflection (15%, about 50 words):** **the decisive part for the top score band.** State how the experience broke a blind spot in your thinking, and explain the specific behaviour you changed afterwards.

### Hooks and cutting the fluff

Admissions reads many similar essays every day. Clichés such as *"I have always been passionate about..."* or *"Since I was young..."* make readers tired within the first three seconds and close their minds to what follows.

*   **Hook strategy:** the first sentence should throw out a concrete turning point, a striking fact or a high-tension scene.
*   **Density:** strip out empty words and buzzwords such as *"strong leadership," "passionate," "hard worker"* and *"great communication skills."* Replace subjective claims with objective facts (show, don't tell), and use strong verbs such as *Initiated, Transformed, Rebuilt, Restructured* and *Spearheaded* to tighten each sentence.

### Before and after

| Dimension | Ordinary expression (score 1–2 tier, fluffy and generic) | Dense, strategic expression (score 4–5 tier, reflective) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Opening hook** | I have always been passionate about business and leadership since I was young, which led me to become president of the club. | When our student club lost $2,000 in school funding two weeks before launch, I had to completely redesign our revenue model overnight. |
| **Translating experience (translation gap)** | I developed strong communication and leadership skills while volunteering at a local animal shelter for two years. | By overhauling our volunteer onboarding workflow, I directly facilitated the adoption of 50 shelter animals and cut application processing time by 50%. |
| **Technology or engineering project (ownership)** | I enjoy building technology and I want to study business and engineering to help people create great applications. | After 3D-printing a custom prosthetic hand for a child in my neighbourhood, I realised the commercial viability of local, low-cost medical manufacturing. |
| **Reflection and growth** | This challenge taught me that teamwork is very important and that a leader should always work hard to achieve goals. | I realised my initial failure came from micromanaging rather than empowering. I shifted from controlling tasks to designing systems, a mindset that governs how I lead teams today. |

## 4. The 2-Minute Prep and 2-Minute Video Interview: A Practical Guide

### How Kira Talent review works: 30,000+ human reviewers

Many applicants assume the video is scored by an algorithm reading keywords or facial expressions. **That isn't the case.**

*   Kira Talent has a global network of **more than 30,000 trained human reviewers**. AI only handles technical checks like spelling. **Every video answer is scored independently by human admissions staff and alumni reviewers.**
*   **Pattern recognition fatigue:** a reviewer may watch hundreds of answers in one season. When a reviewer has watched 40 videos and 32 open with *"I've always been passionate about business..."*, they mentally tune out. **Answers with a clear structure, contrast and real detail are the only thing that breaks through that fatigue.**

### Scoring criteria: the 1–5 tiers

| Assessment dimension | Top-tier performance (score 4–5) | Common deductions (score 1–2) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery on camera** | Eyes fixed on the lens (eye contact), a steady pace (130–150 words per minute), natural, appropriate gestures, warmth and energy, and **deliberate pauses that show live thinking** | Eyes drifting to the screen or notes, a clear sense of memorised text, a flat, monotone voice, and pauses filled with "um" and "ah" |
| **Structure and timing** | A fluent STAR structure, good time awareness, finishing cleanly in about 110–115 seconds, with a very clear through-line | Rambling, no logical thread, running out of time without finishing, or answers of only 30–40 seconds that lack substance |
| **Deep reflection** | Strong self-awareness; a precise account of how the experience changed your habits, your blind spots and your view of leadership | Only a chronological account of events, a slogan-style conclusion ("I learned teamwork is important") or no sign of personal vulnerability |

### Timing a 2-minute answer to the second

```
[00:00 – 00:30] S/T (background and challenge): build the scene in 30 seconds and state the core tension quickly.
[00:30 – 01:30] A (core action): spend 60 seconds on the specific decisions you made, how you handled resistance and why.
[01:30 – 02:00] R/T (result and reflection): finish in 30 seconds with the objective impact and a clear reshaping of your thinking (takeaway).
```

## 5. Dos & Don'ts for the Supplementary Application

### Dos

*   **Sound real, not just impressive:** Smith looks for young people with self-awareness and a growth mindset, not a perfectly polished résumé with no flaws.
*   **Close the translation gap and show impact with data:** back up your contribution with precise numbers and facts ("restructured the process", "raised $2,000", "matched 50 rescue animals").
*   **Practise without a script under real conditions:** rehearse improvised answers with a real camera and timer, with no notes, to build muscle memory on camera and control over time.
*   **Use strong action verbs:** use words like *Spearheaded, Initiated, Transformed, Rebuilt, Engineered* and *Overhauled* to add density to your writing.
*   **Go deep, not wide:** tell one or two stories where you held long-term ownership and went through real conflict and change, rather than listing ten activities.

### Don'ts

*   **Don't read from a script or memorise word for word:** reviewers can spot it within three seconds (small eye movements, flat tone, unnatural pauses). Scripting flattens your voice and puts you in the 1–2 tier.
*   **Don't pile up generic buzzwords and clichés:** never use *"I'm a hard worker," "I developed my communication skills"* or *"I've always been passionate about..."*, which are empty and unsupported.
*   **Don't list every club:** don't cram every club and title you've been involved in into 335 words or two minutes. A list without depth is a classic admission killer.
*   **Don't ask too many people to edit your essay:** too many outside voices dilute your personal voice. Keep one strategy and refine it carefully.

## 6. Academic Integrity, Authenticity and AI Use

> ### ⚠️ Smith's integrity and technical review rules for the supplementary application
>
> *   **Strict anti-copy-paste rules:**
>     In the 10-minute timed written response, Queen's application system uses low-level monitoring. **Pasting text from any pre-written document, clipboard or assistive software is prohibited.** Any copy-paste is flagged immediately by the system and can lead to the loss of your entire application. All text must be typed live, inside the timed interface.
>
> *   **A warning about generative AI (voice flattening):**
>     Relying heavily on generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) to write, generate or over-polish your essay homogenises your language and flattens your voice, erasing its genuine emotion and distinctive tension. Kira Talent reviewers are trained and can easily spot essays that have been heavily processed into an AI template with no real personality behind them. AI can serve as a basic grammar checker, but **the core story, the logic and the reflective insights must come 100% from you.**
>
> *   **Authenticity and verification:**
>     Every story, number and activity you submit must be real and verifiable. Smith reserves the right to spot-check your experiences and run background checks. Honestly showing your real self, your limitations and your growth is the only route through a narrow, roughly 6% admit rate.

## FAQ

### What is Queen's Commerce's admit rate?
About 6%. Many applicants with averages above 98% are turned down because their supplementary application is ordinary.

### What does the written prompt cover?
Random prompts from four main areas: setbacks, uncomfortable feedback, unexpected change, and resourcefulness under constraints. These map to persistence, reflection, adaptability, and initiative with problem solving.

### Can I use ChatGPT for my essay?
Only for basic grammar checks. The story, the logic and the reflection must be entirely yours. Heavy AI polishing makes writing generic, and reviewers notice.

### How long should my video answer be?
Within the two-minute limit, finishing cleanly at around 110–115 seconds is ideal. Speaking for only 30–40 seconds doesn't give enough substance.
