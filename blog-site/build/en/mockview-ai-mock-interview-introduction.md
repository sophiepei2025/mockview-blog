## 1. Why Reading Guides Isn't Enough

The hard part of a Canadian university supplementary application isn't knowing what a good answer looks like. It's doing it under a clock, in one take, in front of human reviewers. Kira-style video questions usually give you one to two minutes to prepare and one to two minutes to answer, with no retakes, and written questions add a word limit and a countdown. Once you understand the rubric, what separates applicants is whether you can actually deliver structure, detail and reflection under real pressure.

Many students practise with a general-purpose chatbot: ask for an interview question, write an answer, ask for feedback. That helps with content, but it's missing three things:

* **No real timing or format.** There's no countdown, no one-take pressure and no school-specific mix of question types.
* **No target-school rubric.** Generic feedback doesn't know what UofT Engineering, McMaster Engineering or Queen's Commerce each look for.
* **A tendency to flatter.** Many general tools score high. You finish practising feeling great, then find the gap on the day.

MockView was built to solve those three problems.

## 2. What MockView Is

**MockView is Edmoat's own AI interview coach for students applying to Canadian undergraduate programs.** You pick a target school and work through that school's real supplementary application format — its question mix, prep time, answer window and word cap — and then receive a report scored on that school's own rubric.

Three design choices stand out:

| Design choice | How it works | What it does for you |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Real format** | Each school has a different mix of question types, prep time, answer length and word limit. MockView follows each school's real format. | The pressure is close to the real thing, so you don't practise one thing and face another. |
| **Scored on that school's rubric, calibrated for score inflation** | It scores on the target school's own dimensions and corrects for the "everyone gets a high score" effect. | Scores sit closer to your real level instead of giving you false comfort. |
| **Quotes your own words, with English you can say out loud** | Feedback quotes what you actually said, points to the specific line that has a problem, and gives every fix as a sentence you can say aloud. | You know what to change and how to say it, with no translating or guessing. |

## 3. What a Report Looks Like

Here is a sample report (from MockView's own example, shown to illustrate the structure):

* **School and prompt:** UofT Engineering, question 2 (a personal experience question): *"Describe a time you had to rethink your approach to a problem. What changed your mind?"*
* **Overall band:** 4 / 5
* **By dimension:**

| Dimension | Score |
| :--- | :---: |
| Program fit | 4 / 5 |
| Narrative & structure | 5 / 5 |
| Specificity | 4 / 5 |
| Reflection & delivery | 3 / 5 |

* **A rewrite suggestion:** for a weak reflection, the report offers a line you can use directly, for example *"What I took away is that good engineering starts by questioning the problem, not the solution."*

As you can see, the report doesn't just give a single number. It shows you **which dimension is weakest** (here, reflection and delivery) and gives you **what to say next time**.

## 5. Who It's For, and Who It Isn't

**It's a good fit for:**

* Students who've read their target school's guide and want to turn knowing into doing.
* Students with little experience of video interviews or timed writing who want a few low-risk rounds first.
* Students for whom English isn't a first language and who want an English fix they can say out loud after every attempt.

**Things to keep in mind:**

* **It's a practice tool, not an admissions predictor.** AI scoring is a practice reference. It isn't the school's real score and can't promise an outcome.
* **It doesn't write your essays or invent your stories.** Your content must come from your own real experience. The fixes help you say it more clearly; they don't make things up for you.
* **For a more personal diagnosis, pair it with a human advisor.** The tool gives you frequent practice; an advisor helps you shape your story bank and application thread.
