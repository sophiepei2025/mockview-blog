## 1. How Kira Talent Assesses You and How Scoring Works

At top Canadian programs such as Waterloo CS/Engineering, UBC Sauder, UofT Engineering, Queen's Health Sciences and Commerce, McMaster BHSc and Western Ivey, the video interview has become the final gatekeeper. When most applicants hold 90%+ or even 95%+ averages, grades are just the ticket in. The video interview is where admissions teams see how you think, how you communicate under pressure and who you really are.

### How scoring works

*   **Who scores you:**
    **First, a common and costly myth: Kira Talent does not use AI to score your video.** AI on the platform only handles technical checks on written answers, such as spelling and grammar. Every video answer is scored independently by **one of 30,000+ trained human reviewers** in Kira's network, calibrated to the specific school's rubric and competencies.
*   **Summed scoring and dual calculation:**
    Reviewers score each competency on a 1–5 scale. Under the newer model, competency scores are **added up (Total Sums Scoring) and calculated twice (Dual Calculation)**. A thin or badly flawed answer on one question or competency drags down your total, and one standout answer can't cover a weak one elsewhere.
*   **Reviewer fatigue and the contrast effect:**
    During admissions season a reviewer may watch dozens or hundreds of videos in a day. After 40 answers, 32 of which open with *"I've always been passionate about..."*, the brain starts to tune out. Answer 33, which opens with **specific detail** and an unexpected angle — a lab vessel cracking and flooding the basement, or negotiating a budget shortfall caused by inflation — stands out sharply. **Specificity can't be faked at scale**, and it is what earns high marks on the scoring sheet.
*   **Scripted answers are penalised:**
    Reviewers are trained to spot memorised or read answers within seconds. When you recite, your voice goes flat, your eyes drift to the screen or a note instead of the lens, your pace turns mechanical and your pauses land in the wrong places. Reviewers put it plainly: "The whole reason we use Kira is to see the real student. When we get scripted responses it's really disappointing." That disappointment turns directly into low scores.

> **From the admissions side:**
> "We don't mind a one-second pause while a student looks for a word. What worries us is a student reciting a perfect 500-word script with empty eyes. Specificity and authenticity can't be manufactured, and they're how you stand out from the other 95% of similar applicants."

### The Kira Talent 1–5 rubric, compared

The table below sets out the 1–5 scoring criteria and what a typical answer sounds like at each level:

| Score | Criteria | Typical answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 (very low)** | Vague or superficial example; little awareness of own contribution; no meaningful reflection or learning. | *"I helped my team sometimes and I learned that leadership is important for everyone."* |
| **2 (at risk)** | Limited detail or doesn't answer the question directly; little appreciation of others' perspectives; mechanical reflection with no real link to growth. | *"We had a group project and someone didn't do the work. So I told the teacher and then we finished it."* |
| **3 (safe)** | Clear, relevant example; reasonable adaptability; reflection includes some insight and links to personal growth. | *"When our team lagged behind, I created a shared presentation sheet and helped resolve the disagreement. I learned how to manage team timelines better."* |
| **4 (competitive)** | Thoughtful, specific example; strong initiative, adaptability and respect for others' perspectives; clear reflection on how behaviour and outlook changed. | *"I planned our study schedule after noticing my team falling behind. I realized I needed to listen to their individual constraints rather than micromanaging, which kept us on track."* |
| **5 (top tier)** | Vivid, authentic and highly reflective; maturity, self-direction and composure under pressure; deep reflection on a real shift in thinking and behaviour. | *"When our project failed, I realized I focused on control rather than collaboration. I adjusted how I led, trusted my team, and saw our next project succeed. That experience changed how I lead every group today."* |

## 2. The Core Protocol: Five Steps to Every Answer

With 1–2 minutes to prepare, 1–2 minutes to answer and no redos, your thinking and delivery need to become muscle memory. Follow this five-step protocol every time:

```
[1: Read the prompt] -> [2: Name the competency] -> [3: Pick a story] -> [4: Build 3 beats / STAR] -> [5: Deliver to the lens]
```

### 2.1 Step 1: Read and analyse
In the 1–2 minutes of prep, strip away noise and find what the question is really asking:
*   **Behavioural:** a real past experience ("Tell us about a time you…").
*   **Situational:** a hypothetical conflict ("If you were faced with…, what would you do?").
*   **On-the-spot thinking / analytical:** reasoning and explaining a concept (such as UofT Engineering's variable-analysis prompts).

### 2.2 Step 2: Name the competency
Map the question to a Kira competency:
*   **Leadership & Initiative**
*   **Critical Thinking**
*   **Communication Clarity**
*   **Problem Solving**
*   **Adaptability & Resilience**
*   **Self-Reflection**
*   **Program Fit**

### 2.3 Step 3: Pick a story
Pull the best match from your prepared story bank. **Don't force an impressive but unrelated achievement into the answer**; choose the real story that shows the competency most clearly.

### 2.4 Step 4: Build three beats / STAR+R
Never ramble. For a 2-minute answer, split your time like this:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                   STAR+R timing and structure                                       |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Beat                              | Share of time and what to deliver                             |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| S (Situation)                     | • 10%–15% (about 15 seconds)                                  |
|                                   | • Set the scene with the 5 Ws, fast. No long build-up.        |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| T (Task)                          | • 10% (about 10 seconds)                                      |
|                                   | • Name the core friction, constraint or conflict.             |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| A (Action)                        | • 50% (about 60 seconds)                                      |
|                                   | • The heart of the answer: the steps you took, why, how you   |
|                                   |   handled stakeholders and friction. Show, don't tell.        |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| R (Result)                        | • 10% (about 15 seconds)                                      |
|                                   | • Clear outcomes and measurable numbers.                      |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| +R (Takeaway / Reflection)        | • 20% (about 20 seconds)                                      |
|                                   | • What separates a 3 from a 5: how your thinking and          |
|                                   |   behaviour changed.                                          |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **Weak reflection vs. deep reflection:**
>
> *   ❌ **Ordinary reflection (score 3):**
>     *"I learned that communication is very important in a team, and I learned to work harder next time."* (A common-sense lesson with no personal insight.)
> *   💡 **Deep reflection (score 5):**
>     *"I realized I had been treating leadership as giving directions when I actually needed to create space for others to contribute. Since then, I transitioned from micromanaging tasks to establishing psychological safety first—a framework I now bring to every collaborative project."* (Names a flaw in your own behaviour and a repeatable change.)

### 2.5 Step 5: Deliver with confidence
Breathe, look into the lens, and deliver your structure with energy and a steady pace.

## 3. Building Your Story Bank: 5–8 Core Stories

Questions are random, so don't improvise from scratch. Build an **experience bank** of 5–8 strong stories in advance.

### The eight scenarios your bank must cover

1.  **Leadership & Initiative:** stepping up and driving change without a title or with almost no resources.
2.  **Teamwork & Perspective-Taking:** understanding teammates from different backgrounds and reaching agreement.
3.  **Conflict & Disagreement:** working through interpersonal tension or a technical disagreement.
4.  **Unexpected Change & Adaptability:** adjusting quickly when plans fall apart.
5.  **Mistakes, Failure & Difficult Feedback:** honest vulnerability, self-awareness and what you changed.
6.  **Resourcefulness under Constraints:** finding a way through with very tight budget, people or time.
7.  **Composure under Pressure:** staying calm and making a sound call at a critical moment.
8.  **A Pivotal Learning Moment:** a time a belief was broken and you rebuilt how you think.

### The T-model for your stories

Build your bank in a T-shape so it has both depth and range:
*   **Depth (1–2 anchor activities):** long-term experiences where you had real ownership, such as launching a social initiative, running a profitable small business or leading a charity challenge.
*   **Breadth (3–4 supporting activities):** a part-time job crisis, friction while organising a club event, athletic training — enough range to fit any competency.

---

### Show, don't tell: three rewrites

Skip statements like "I showed great leadership." Below, Edmoat's compiled high-scoring examples show how a vague statement becomes a measurable, reflective 5-point story.

#### Example 1: Fixing an animal shelter's adoption process
*   ❌ **BEFORE (telling — score 2):**
    > *"I volunteered at a local animal shelter and helped them out with animal adoptions. I showed a lot of leadership and communication skills, which made the shelter very happy and taught me how to work hard."*
*   ✅ **AFTER (showing — score 5):**
    > *"When volunteering at the regional shelter, I noticed the adoption drop-off rate was over 40% due to a paper-heavy 5-page application form that overwhelmed prospective families. **[Challenge]** I initiated a workflow overhaul, designing a streamlined 2-minute digital match assessment and training 15 volunteers on a preliminary interview protocol. **[Action & Stakeholders]** Within three months, we successfully matched 50 shelter animals into verified permanent homes while reducing intake bottlenecks by 35%. **[Quantified Result]** This taught me that effective leadership isn't about working harder within a broken system, but taking the initiative to diagnose systemic friction and build sustainable solutions." **[Deep Reflection]***

#### Example 2: Merging team budgets during inflation
*   ❌ **BEFORE (telling — score 2):**
    > *"Our student council had a budget issue because of inflation. I helped solve the conflict between team captains and we ended up throwing a nice end-of-year party."*
*   ✅ **AFTER (showing — score 5):**
    > *"In 2023, rising inflation meant the student council's traditional $200 budget per athletic team was no longer enough for individual end-of-year celebrations, leading to heated arguments among team captains competing for extra funding. **[Challenge]** I brought all 10 captains into a structured roundtable, acknowledging their frustration, and proposed consolidating our ten separate $200 budgets into a single $2,000 joint athletic gala. **[Action & Stakeholders]** I negotiated shared vendor rates and ensured every team had equal representation in the award program. The event hosted 250+ athletes with a 98% satisfaction score. **[Quantified Result]** I realized true conflict resolution requires shifting stakeholders from fighting over scarce resources to reimagining how shared resources can create greater collective value." **[Deep Reflection]***

#### Example 3: 31 days of Murph to fundraise for MS
*   ❌ **BEFORE (telling — score 2):**
    > *"I decided to raise money for Multiple Sclerosis because my dad has it. I did a really hard workout every day and raised a lot of money on GoFundMe."*
*   ✅ **AFTER (showing — score 5):**
    > *"Driven by my father's 10-year battle with Multiple Sclerosis, I launched 'Murph for MS'—a fundraising campaign centered on completing the extreme Murph workout (1-mile run, 100 pull-ups, 200 push-ups, 300 squats, 1-mile run) every single day for 31 consecutive days in July. **[Challenge]** Facing severe physical fatigue and low initial donation traction, I built a strategic outreach plan: creating a digital training module for local gyms and pitching 200 CrossFit affiliates across Canada. **[Action & Stakeholders]** Five major gyms joined as official hubs, mobilizing hundreds of athletes on a national 'Murph Day', expanding our GoFundMe campaign from a $2,000 local target to over $20,000 raised for the MS Society of Canada. **[Quantified Result]** This experience transformed my understanding of advocacy: inspiring action isn't about asking for sympathy, but embodying resilience so visibly that it creates a movement others feel compelled to join." **[Deep Reflection]***

## 4. On-Camera Presence and Delivery

A video interview needs more than good material: you have to come across well on camera.

### Five elements of delivery

*   **Eye contact:** look at the **camera lens**, not at yourself or the countdown. That's what creates eye contact and sincerity on the reviewer's side.
*   **Energy:** keep a confident, varied tone. The camera absorbs about 20% of your expressiveness, so bring 10%–15% more energy than in normal conversation.
*   **Clarity:** speak clearly with a visible structure, using transitions like *First, Second, Ultimately*.
*   **Expression:** let your face match the story — focused when describing the challenge, a natural smile at the breakthrough.
*   **Gestures:** use natural gestures within a mid-shot frame to emphasise points, without constant movement.

### Pacing and the deliberate pause

Many applicants fill every second out of nerves, packing answers with *um, uh, like, you know*. Remember: **a deliberate one-second pause signals composure, confidence and depth**; rushing and fillers signal anxiety.

> **From the admissions side:**
> "When you get a hard question, look at the camera and say, *'That's a thoughtful question. Let me structure my approach to this...'*, then pause for two seconds to organise your thoughts. That maturity and composure under pressure scores a full band higher than rushing into a stream of disorganised filler."

### Setup checklist

- [ ] **Quiet space:** a private room with no family, pets or background noise.
- [ ] **Lighting:** light from the front (a ring light or facing a window); avoid backlight or harsh overhead light.
- [ ] **Connection and gear:** a stable, fast connection with bandwidth-heavy apps closed; an HD webcam raised to eye level.
- [ ] **Professional look:** business casual, with a tidy, simple background.

## 5. The 50+ Recording Method

A video interview is a muscle-memory skill. Train intensively with tools such as Edmoat's MockView AI mock interview.

### Practice rules
Match real Kira conditions exactly: **no redos**, 1–2 minutes to think and 1–2 minutes to answer, so your brain adapts to the pressure.

### The daily four-step loop

```
[1. Record under real timing]
         │
         ▼
[2. Review the scores and metrics]
         │
         ▼
[3. Rework the structure and reflection]
         │
         ▼
[4. Record again]
```

Run this loop on 3–5 questions a day. Over seven days that's 20–30 different questions and 50+ recordings, enough to move from a 1 to a 5.

### Seven-day progression

| Days | Focus | Expected score |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Days 1–2** | Get used to timed prep and answers and overcome camera nerves; build a basic structure so you finish within time. | **1–2** (eliminate vague, superficial answers) |
| **Days 3–4** | Audit filler words and improve transitions; tighten the STAR chain and your timing. | **3** (into the safe zone) |
| **Days 5–6** | Deepen reflection (+R); strengthen ownership, stakeholder communication and self-awareness. | **4** (competitive) |
| **Day 7 onward** | Random-question pressure tests; natural, unscripted, memorable delivery. | **4–5** (top tier) |

### Metrics to review

Compare against your MockView score report and check these four things yourself:

1.  **Vocal clarity:** clear pronunciation and a steady pace.
2.  **Filler word percentage:** how often you say *um, uh, like*. **Aim for under 2%.**
3.  **Time utilisation:** use **80%–90%** of the time (for a 2-minute answer, 1:40 to 1:50) — not so short you look thin, not so long your reflection gets cut off.
4.  **Structure and depth:** a complete STAR structure, with at least 20% of the time on genuine reflection.

## 6. Kira Talent Dos & Don'ts

| Dos | Don'ts |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Strategy:** start weeks ahead and use rolling admissions to practise often. | **Strategy:** wait until the night before the deadline with no timed practice. |
| **Authenticity:** be real and willing to show vulnerability — real mistakes, real friction and what they taught you. | **Authenticity:** build a flawless persona out of awards and achievements with no self-awareness. |
| **Structure:** follow STAR plus a real takeaway, keep the situation under 15%, and make actions specific and reflection deep. | **Structure:** ramble chronologically and spend half your time on the situation so the key content gets cut off. |
| **Language:** use specific detail and numbers (show, don't tell), and a deliberate pause instead of panicked filler. | **Language:** rely on grand, empty phrases like *"I am a passionate leader"* or *"I developed communication skills"*. |
| **Camera:** look at the lens with a natural, confident expression and moderate gestures. | **Camera:** watch the countdown, let your eyes wander, or read from notes beside the screen — reviewers spot it instantly. |
| **Advice:** keep a single source of truth — one consistent, professional strategy and coach. | **Advice:** ask too many people (parents, teachers, Reddit, friends) and lose your own voice in conflicting edits. |
