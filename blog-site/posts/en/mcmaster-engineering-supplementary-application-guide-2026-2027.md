## 1. McMaster Engineering: The Application Process

In Canada's top engineering admissions, the weight of grades is slowly shifting. With so many applicants above 92%–95%, a very high average is only the minimum needed to keep a file from being filtered out automatically (a box to tick). What decides whether you stand out among tens of thousands of candidates and receive an offer is the depth of your engineering thinking, your potential for teamwork and the distinctive character of your story in the supplementary application.

### Engineering values and the T-shaped activity model

McMaster Engineering places great value on students who turn theory into real-world impact. Its first-year core course, **Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects in Engineering**, asks students to work in a "collaborative learning environment which fosters community," proposing "concrete solutions for real-world problems in our communities."

So before you submit, rethink your experiences with the **T-shaped activity model**:
*   **The horizontal bar is breadth:** show interests and basic skills across different fields (for example, a math contest, playing in the school band, a community job or peer tutoring).
*   **The vertical bar is depth:** choose 1–2 long-term projects with real engineering or technical iteration and community impact. For example, two years as a core member of a robotics team or structural design project, repeatedly prototyping after a structural failure, and ultimately solving a practical problem for the community.

### Application timeline

*   **October: planning and positioning (a clear strategic advantage from starting early)**
    *   Start planning and define your engineering story, rather than scrambling near the January or February deadline.
    *   Study the collaborative, community-focused teaching model of McMaster's first-year Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects, and tie your experience to the school's approach to solving real community problems.
    *   Map your T-model and pull out the technical conflicts and teamwork friction points in your core engineering projects.
*   **December–January: material refinement and realistic practice**
    *   Build a personal **experience bank**, with real examples of failure, limited resources, disagreement and changed thinking.
    *   Use a **single source of truth**: work with one professional advisor or a single consistent framework. Don't ask parents, friends, teachers and Reddit for conflicting opinions. Multiple rounds of feedback can mean seven or more rewrites, which strip out your distinctive voice and fill the essay with buzzwords.
    *   Rehearse on camera with an interview simulator, monitoring eye focus, tone variation, energy, clarity and gestures.
*   **February: final checks and submission (finish calmly before the deadline)**
    *   Before the official February deadline, pick a time with a stable connection and a quiet room, and complete the 20-minute online recording and submission in one sitting.

### How typical applicants and strategic applicants compare

## 2. The 20-Minute Supplementary Application (One Essay + Three Video Questions)

McMaster Engineering's supplementary application runs on the Kira Talent platform and takes about 20 minutes. It includes one timed written essay and three live video questions. The system offers no pausing, retakes or withdrawals.

### How Kira Talent assessment works

To score well, you need to understand how the platform works:

1. **A clear split between AI screening and human scoring:** the platform's AI is used **only** for technical checks on the written essay, such as spelling, grammar and text structure. The video answers are assessed **100% by human reviewers**.
2. **A large, trained reviewer network:** Kira has more than 30,000 professionals trained to score independently, using each university's competency rubric on a 1–5 scale.
3. **Reviewer psychology and pattern recognition:** reviewers watch dozens or even hundreds of candidates in a single application cycle (one review session can include as many as 40 candidates). Under that fatigue, they unconsciously start to recognise patterns: if they hear 30 people open with "I've always been passionate about engineering," they become seriously tired of it. **The way to break that fatigue and win back attention is to open with concrete detail and a hook that creates a visual or mental contrast.**
4. **The fatal cost of reciting a script:** reviewers are explicitly trained to spot "scripted reading." Once you start reciting a pre-written answer, your eye movements (staring at the screen or drifting), your flattened tone and your unnatural pauses give you away instantly. That reads as a lack of authenticity and drags your overall score down heavily.

### The structure of the supplementary application

> **[Stage 1: preparation]** Equipment checks and setting up your environment (camera at eye level, network test, mental preparation)
>     │
>     ▼
> **[Stage 2: Module 1, the timed written essay]** One essay, completed within the time limit, with an AI spelling and grammar check
>     ├─ **Hook:** the core conflict or unexpected moment (about 10%)
>     ├─ **Context:** the 5 Ws, quickly (about 25%)
>     ├─ **Action:** the specific engineering steps and your personal contribution (about 45%)
>     └─ **Reflection:** the deep shift in how you think (about 20%)
>     │
>     ▼
> **[Stage 3: Module 2, the video interview]** Three video questions in a row, each scored independently by a human reviewer
>     ├─ **Video question 1:** thinking time (recording starts automatically when prep ends) ➔ the recording (hold eye contact with the lens and keep your energy up)
>     ├─ **Video question 2:** thinking time (use STAR or RPI to structure your logic) ➔ the recording (focus on specific Proof and Insight)
>     └─ **Video question 3:** thinking time (stay composed) ➔ the recording (natural pauses and a clear summary)
>     │
>     ▼
> **[Stage 4: confirm and submit]** Check that all data has uploaded, then complete the application.

### Module details

#### 1. Module 1: the written essay
*   **Core rule:** be extremely concise and cut every piece of filler and unnecessary rhetoric (cut the fluff).
*   **Structure:** get to the point. After a quick background, give most of your words to the specific engineering actions you took (Action) and the breakthrough in self-understanding the experience produced (Reflection).

#### 2. Module 2: the three video questions
*   **How it works:** the questions are drawn at random. The system times your preparation and starts recording automatically. It tests your logical reasoning and improvised delivery under pressure.
*   **The five elements of on-camera delivery:**
    1. **Eye contact:** look straight into the camera lens (not at yourself on screen) to create a strong sense of conversation with the reviewer.
    2. **Presence and energy:** keep a full, confident warmth and avoid a stiff or flat tone.
    3. **Clarity:** speak at a moderate pace and articulate clearly, controlling unintentional fillers such as "um" and "ah."
    4. **Expression:** keep a natural, open expression that shows emotional control.
    5. **Gestures:** use appropriate natural gestures within the frame to make your logic more persuasive.

## 3. The Core Expression Framework: Combining RPI and STAR

To give a convincing answer in a very short time for thinking and recording, combine the **STAR method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) with the **RPI framework** (Reference, Proof, Insight).

### Breaking down RPI

*   **Reference (the context you're drawing on):** quickly establish a credible, specific identity and team context. Don't rely on abstract claims like "I'm a leader." Name the actual team (a robotics competition team, a community water-resource design project, or your preparation for McMaster's Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects).
*   **Proof (facts and quantified evidence):** avoid vague buzzwords like "improved leadership" or "strengthened communication." Use concrete actions, engineering parameters and data: how many team members you coached, the specific structural failure you overcame, or the percentage weight you cut through a geometric redesign.
*   **Insight (a deep shift in your thinking):** **this is the key to reaching the 4 or 5 band.** A strong insight is not a surface lesson like "I learned that teamwork matters." It's a fundamental change in your mental model or perspective (a shift in perspective): how your view of leadership, risk control or teamwork changed after the experience.

### A high-scoring model answer, with sentence-by-sentence analysis

> **Scenario:** answering a question about an engineering challenge, a team conflict or facing a failure, in either the written essay or the video.
>
> **[Model high-scoring answer]**
> "In Grade 11, while leading a 5-member robotics team for a regional structural design challenge, we faced a total loss of structural integrity during stress testing just 48 hours before competition. **(Situation & Task / Reference)** Instead of enforcing my original blueprint, I paused our work, called a 10-minute brainstorming session, and redistributed testing tasks based on team members' expertise in load distribution. **(Action & Proof)** We redesigned the support truss using an iterated triangular geometric pattern, reducing total material weight by 15% while sustaining the target load. **(Result & Proof)** This failure reshaped my understanding of engineering teamwork: I realized that under high-stakes pressure, effective leadership isn't about maintaining control over an initial design, but about creating an adaptable space where every team member feels safe to contribute critical solutions. **(Insight / Shift in Perspective)** I look forward to bringing this collaborative, community-building mindset to McMaster's Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects."

#### Sentence-by-sentence analysis

1. *"In Grade 11, while leading a 5-member robotics team for a regional structural design challenge, we faced a total loss of structural integrity during stress testing just 48 hours before competition."*
   * **Analysis (Situation/Task + Reference):** a clear background reference, with the specific time, a five-person team, and a real conflict with strong visual tension: the structure failed completely 48 hours before the competition.
2. *"Instead of enforcing my original blueprint, I paused our work, called a 10-minute brainstorming session, and redistributed testing tasks based on team members' expertise in load distribution."*
   * **Analysis (Action + Proof):** drops the empty claim "I showed excellent leadership" and proves the point with specific actions (pausing work, a 10-minute brainstorm, reassigning tasks by load-distribution expertise).
3. *"We redesigned the support truss using an iterated triangular geometric pattern, reducing total material weight by 15% while sustaining the target load."*
   * **Analysis (Result + Proof):** shows hard engineering thinking (an iterated triangular geometry) and objective numbers (15% lighter while holding the target load), directly demonstrating concrete problem-solving ability to the assessor.
4. *"This failure reshaped my understanding of engineering teamwork: I realized that under high-stakes pressure, effective leadership isn't about maintaining control over an initial design, but about creating an adaptable space where every team member feels safe to contribute critical solutions."*
   * **Analysis (Insight, the shift in thinking):** shows mature self-examination. It moves from "executing a task from outside" to "a real change inside," from "forcing control over the original design" to "creating a space where everyone can safely contribute solutions." That's a textbook 5-point insight.
5. *"I look forward to bringing this collaborative, community-building mindset to McMaster's Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects."*
   * **Analysis (program fit):** a natural link back to the question. It connects personal growth seamlessly to McMaster's core course, which emphasises collaboration and community building.

## 4. Calm Under Pressure and Logical Reasoning: Dos & Don'ts

For the timed essay and the improvised video, follow these principles so your answers come across as real, specific and logically compelling.

| Dimension | Dos (the right approach) | Don'ts (pitfalls to avoid) | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Opening and narrative** | **Do:** open with a specific unexpected moment, an engineering failure or a particular detail (for example, "Two days before the competition, our structure collapsed during stress testing"). | **Don't:** open with clichés like "I've always been passionate about engineering" or "Since I was young." | A reviewer may see up to 40 videos in one session. Generic clichés trigger fatigue instantly and put you in the "forgettable" pile. A concrete moment of conflict creates contrast and pulls attention back. |
| **Conciseness** | **Do:** get straight to the point; focus on **one** deep engineering experience; cut every meaningless modifier and unnecessary background (cut the fluff). | **Don't:** try to fit every club you've joined (student council, sports teams, volunteering, contest titles) into 200 words or two minutes. | Listing clubs makes the answer shallow and scattered. Real depth in a single experience (its internal shift) beats empty breadth every time. |
| **On-camera delivery** | **Do:** keep your eyes on the lens, stay natural in expression and gesture, keep a steady pace, and show confidence and energy. | **Don't:** recite a script, stare at the screen, let your eyes drift, or deliver a flat, stiff tone. | The purpose of the Kira review is to judge the "real person." Scripting shifts eye movement and flattens tone, and reviewers will mark down authenticity right away. |
| **Handling pressure** | **Do:** reason through the problem calmly with a visible structure (STAR or RPI), admit a limitation honestly and offer a measured reflection. | **Don't:** panic at an unfamiliar question, or force a prepared answer that doesn't fit the new question. | The core of the assessment is whether you can "think clearly and express yourself under pressure." Logical reasoning and calm composure are more convincing than a forced template. |
| **Feedback loop** | **Do:** build a **single source of truth** and get systematic calibration from one professional advisor or a single framework. | **Don't:** collect conflicting feedback from parents, teachers, friends and Reddit, or rewrite the essay seven times. | Conflicting edits create "admission killers": each person's changes strip out your unique voice and leave the writing generic. |

## 5. A Seven-Day Study Plan and Simulation Guide

Following the approach of moving a video interview from a 1 to a 4–5 within seven days, aim to complete about **20 practice sessions**, climbing step by step with this plan:

1. **Days 1–2: build your experience bank (rough phase, moving from scores of 1–2)**
   * **Core task:** review the last 2–3 years of core experiences and pull out 3–4 real stories with **friction** (failure, limited resources, disagreement).
   * **Practice target:** complete 4–5 basic recordings in the simulator. At this stage answers tend to be surface-level (a 1–2 score: they describe what was done but lack personal contribution and depth of thinking). The goal is to turn your experiences into material you can draw on.
2. **Days 3–4: blend the frameworks and sharpen your writing (logic phase, moving toward a 3)**
   * **Core task:** rework your stories with STAR and RPI. Cut every empty PR phrase line by line (such as "strong leadership" or "great communication skills") and replace it with concrete **Proof** (data and actions) and **Insight** (changed thinking).
   * **Practice target:** complete 5–6 timed written essays and video drills. Make sure each answer reaches the safe zone (a 3: a complete structure, clear logic and reasonable adaptability with basic reflection).
3. **Days 5–6: camera rehearsal and presence (breakthrough phase, moving to 4–5)**
   * **Core task:** run camera rehearsals or use the interview simulator to check eye focus (on the lens), tonal variation, natural pauses and gestures. Focus on turning a plain "event summary" into a deep shift in self-understanding (a shift in perspective).
   * **Practice target:** complete 8–10 improvised drills on random questions. When you replay them, check whether you stay calm with unfamiliar questions and end with a mature insight (a 4–5 answer shows clear personal ownership, deep self-examination and composure under pressure).
4. **Day 7: full simulation and mindset check (final submission)**
   * **Core task:** run the entire process in one go, respecting the real 20-minute limit (one timed essay plus three video questions).
   * **Practice target:** after your last full simulation, check your equipment and connection, get yourself into the best state, and calmly complete the official Kira Talent submission.

## FAQ

### What does McMaster Engineering's supplementary application include?
It's completed on the Kira platform in about 20 minutes: one timed written essay and three improvised video questions. There's no pausing, retaking or withdrawing.

### What is the RPI framework?
Reference (establish the specific team and situation), Proof (back up your points with actions and data) and Insight (explain how your perspective changed). It's usually combined with STAR.

### How do I show I understand McMaster in my answer?
Connect your experience to the first-year Integrated Cornerstone Design Projects. The course emphasises working collaboratively to propose concrete solutions to real community problems.

### When is the best time to submit?
Before the official February deadline, in one sitting, at a quiet time with a stable connection. Check the official dates each year.
