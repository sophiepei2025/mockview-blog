> **Short answer:** The Rotman Commerce supplementary application has four parts: a prepared 200-word activity essay and 60-second video, plus a random 10-minute, 200-word essay and a live video (usually 90 seconds to think, 60 to answer). Reviewers score leadership, critical thinking, communication, composure and business fit.

**Key points**

- Four parts: known essay (200 words), known video (60s), random essay (10 min, 200 words), random video.
- 60-second video: 0–10s hook → 10–45s action and analysis → 45–60s result and reflection.
- Highlight business and technical tools: Excel models, Python, SQL, A/B tests.
- In 200 words, focus on one core role instead of five or six activities.
- At least 20 practice runs in 7 days; keep fillers under two per minute.

## Part 1: How the Rotman Commerce Supplementary Application Works

The Rotman Commerce supplementary application runs on Kira Talent. It has two components you can prepare in advance and two that are generated live under pressure:

```
                Rotman Commerce supplementary application at a glance
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  Rotman Commerce supplementary application               │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                   │
       ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
       ▼                                                       ▼
┌───────────────┐                                       ┌───────────────┐
│  Prepared     │                                       │  Random/live  │
└───────┬───────┘                                       └───────┬───────┘
        │                                                       │
        ├─► 1. Known activity essay                             ├─► 3. Random live essay
        │   └─ 200 words | plan and refine in advance           │   └─ 10-minute limit | 200 words | written live
        │                                                       │
        └─► 2. Known commercial video                           └─► 4. Random live video
            └─ 60 seconds | plan and polish delivery                └─ 90s to think / 60s to answer | Kira, improvised
```

> **How the four components work**
> 1. **Known activity essay (200 words):** a tight story and reflection about one core extracurricular activity.
> 2. **Known commercial video (60 seconds):** a high-impact 60-second answer to a set business or personal theme.
> 3. **Random essay (200 words, 10 minutes):** a randomly drawn prompt answered within a 10-minute countdown.
> 4. **Random video interview (improvised):** a randomly drawn question with set thinking time (usually 90 seconds), then a 60-second recorded answer.

### How Kira Talent review works

Many applicants believe Kira Talent videos are scored by AI or keyword scanning. **That's a costly misconception.**

*   **Human reviewers:** your video answers are watched and scored independently by Kira Talent's network of more than 30,000 trained human reviewers, each calibrated to the competencies Rotman Commerce specifies.
*   **The competency rubric:** reviewers score five core areas:
    *   *Leadership potential:* initiative, motivating a team and taking responsibility.
    *   *Critical thinking and problem solving:* reasoning and handling complex situations.
    *   *Communication clarity and delivery:* structured storytelling, pace and eye contact.
    *   *Composure and adaptability:* staying calm with unfamiliar questions.
    *   *Genuine program fit:* business awareness and personal sincerity.
*   **Summed and weighted scoring:** the platform adds up scores from several independent reviewers in a weighted aggregate. A serious weakness in one competency (such as no real reflection) drags down the overall score, and each question is assessed as a separate unit.
*   **Pattern recognition:** reviewers watch hundreds of videos in a cycle and can spot generic or scripted answers within the first five seconds. A script shows in a flat voice, darting eyes and stiff pauses, and is marked down heavily.

## Part 2: Business and Technical Orientation, and the T-Model

Admissions doesn't value the number of activities. It's looking for applicants with clear intent, real depth and business or technical sense.

```
                       T-model framework
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Breadth: 2–3 secondary supporting activities                            │
│ (varied interests and soft skills: adaptability, teamwork, communication) │
└────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     │ Depth
                                     ▼
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ 1–2 anchor projects / passion project │
                  │ Long-term commitment | deep leadership │
                  │ Business and tech tools | measurable impact │
                  └─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Breadth and depth

*   **Breadth:** choose 2–3 secondary activities (a school team, club volunteering). You don't need to cover everything; show varied interests and general soft skills.
*   **Depth:** choose 1–2 anchor commitments or self-started leadership projects, with emphasis on long-term commitment and projects you started from intrinsic motivation.

### Showing business and technical orientation

Rotman Commerce values business analysis, data insight and practical use of technical tools. When describing activities, don't stop at administrative duties; show how you used technology or business analysis to solve real problems.

#### 1. Data tools and analysis
Build specific tools into your story (Excel financial models, Python data cleaning, SQL queries, social media conversion analysis, survey data) to show business sense:

*   *Abstract (avoid):* "I handled the club's finances and promotion and raised its profile."
*   *Business- and tech-focused (recommended):* "I built an Excel forecasting model from three years of club fundraising data and used social media A/B tests to optimise our posts, raising event conversion by 35% and $2,000 in funds."

#### 2. Show, don't tell: rewriting real examples

Drawing on real admissions cases, here's how to upgrade ordinary activities into high-impact business and technical stories:

*   **Example 1: business analysis and social impact (charity fundraising)**
    *   *Wrong (tell):* "I'm very passionate and charitable; I did fitness activities and raised money for charity."
    *   *Right (show):* launched the "Murph for MS" challenge, completing the gruelling Murph workout (1-mile run + 100 pull-ups + 200 push-ups + 300 squats + 1-mile run) every day for 31 days; built an online donation tracker and partnered with five CrossFit gyms across Canada to raise more than $20,000 for multiple sclerosis research.
*   **Example 2: operations and measurable impact (community service)**
    *   *Wrong (tell):* "I volunteered at an animal shelter and developed strong communication and leadership skills."
    *   *Right (show):* redesigned the shelter's online matching process and pet database, segmented adopter preference data, and matched 50 animals with new homes in 60 days.
*   **Example 3: technology and social innovation (engineering/tech)**
    *   *Wrong (tell):* "I love building tech products and I'm interested in engineering and business."
    *   *Right (show):* used 3D printing and open-source modelling software to design and make a low-cost prosthetic hand for a child with a disability in the community, then worked with a local clinic to explore small-scale production.
*   **Example 4: crisis leadership (club operations)**
    *   *Wrong (tell):* "I'm president of the school business club and a strong leader."
    *   *Right (show):* when the club lost all its funding to school budget cuts, rebuilt the sponsorship plan, approached eight local businesses for brand partnerships and restored the club's funding.
*   **Example 5: work ethic and resilience (personal background)**
    *   *Wrong (tell):* "I work very hard and can handle a lot of pressure."
    *   *Right (show):* kept top grades and studied for exams while working night shifts at the family restaurant until after midnight, building strong time management and calm under pressure.

#### 3. The written fluff penalty
Many applicants try to fit student council, sports, volunteering, tutoring and music — five or six things — into 200 words. **This makes the answer shallow and forgettable, and human reviewers judge it scattered.** Strip it back and analyse **one core role** in depth.

## Part 3: Breaking Down the Four Components

### 3.1 Component 1: the known activity essay (200 words)

*   **Cut the fluff:** 200 words is very tight. Remove every stock phrase ("Since I was young…", "I've always been passionate about…").
*   **No buzzwords:** never just claim "I am a strong leader" or "I have excellent communication skills." Prove it with specific actions and metrics.
*   **A catalyst and a mindset change:** the story must include a turning point and how it changed your understanding (for example, from focusing on your own performance to empowering your teammates).

### 3.2 Component 2: the known commercial video (60 seconds)

A 60-second known video needs a second-by-second plan:

```
60-second known video structure:
[0–10s] High-impact hook ──> [10–45s] Core business action and analysis ──> [45–60s] Quantified result and deep reflection
```

#### Three hook formulas
1.  **Anecdotal turning point:** "When our project's funding was cut to zero 48 hours before the event, I realised the traditional sponsorship model had failed."
2.  **Startling metric:** "Matching 50 rescue animals to the right families in 30 days took more than kindness; it took data segmentation."
3.  **Counter-intuitive observation:** "I used to think leadership meant controlling everything, until a failed project taught me real leadership is delegating and trusting."

> ⚠️ **A serious delivery warning**
>
> Human reviewers are very good at spotting reading from the screen or reciting a script. Scripting causes a flat voice, avoided eye contact and a stiff tone. **Once judged as scripted, you're placed in the lowest band.** Keep your eyes on the lens, with natural energy and moderate gestures.

### 3.3 Component 3: the random essay (200 words, 10 minutes)

Under Kira's 10-minute limit, follow a **minute-by-minute plan**:

```
10-minute random essay plan:
[00:00–02:00] Read the prompt ──> set your core thesis ──> pick an anchor story from your experience bank
[02:00–08:00] Write ──> short sentences ──> action verbs and numbers
[08:00–10:00] Polish ──> cut extra words ──> read it silently for flow ──> submit
```

*   **Minute by minute:**
    *   *Minutes 1–2 (plan):* identify what the prompt is really about (handling conflict, overcoming failure, allocating resources) and lock in **one** best-fit story from your experience bank.
    *   *Minutes 3–8 (write):* go straight to the point and use situation–action–result. No filler.
    *   *Minutes 9–10 (check):* fix grammar and spelling, trim pointless adjectives and make sure the logic flows.

### 3.4 Component 4: the random video interview (improvised Kira)

Use **STAR** to stay clear within a 60-second answer:

*   **S – Situation (10 seconds):** brief context (who, what, when, where, why, how).
*   **T – Task (5 seconds):** the specific challenge or goal.
*   **A – Action (30 seconds):** **the core.** The analysis, decisions and actions you personally took.
*   **R – Result & Takeaway (15 seconds):** the quantified outcome, and **always a real change in your thinking**.

#### Kira Talent scoring: low vs. high

| | 1–2 (avoid) | 4–5 (aim for) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Specificity** | Abstract and vague, generic language ("I joined a project and learned communication and leadership"), no data or detail. | A specific, vivid story; your distinct contribution, decisions and quantified results in a complex situation. |
| **Delivery and authenticity** | Reciting a script or reading the screen, a flat stiff voice, avoiding the lens, lots of "um/ah." | Eyes on the lens, sincere and confident tone, energy and natural gestures; calm under pressure with good pacing. |
| **Leadership shift** | Passive, or confusing leadership with micromanaging and controlling others; blaming others for failure. | Strong initiative; **from micromanaging to motivating; from control to collaboration.** |
| **Self-awareness and reflection** | No reflection or shallow clichés ("I learned the importance of hard work"); can't explain what changed. | High self-awareness; **clearly analyses how your thinking was rebuilt after a failure and how that lesson shapes how you act now.** |

## Part 4: Rotman Commerce Dos & Don'ts

### Essays and storytelling

*   **Do:**
    *   Open with a specific, striking personal anecdote as your hook.
    *   Show results with precise numbers and action verbs ("built an Excel model to reorganise a 25-person team's workload").
    *   Show how a key event changed your mindset.
*   **Don't:**
    *   Open with "Since I was young…" or "I've always been passionate about…".
    *   Turn your essay into an activity dump, cramming everything into 200 words.
    *   Simply call yourself a "strong leader" or "hard worker" with no story behind it.

### Video interviews and delivery

*   **Do:**
    *   Stay composed; natural pauses while you think are fine.
    *   Build a modular experience bank in advance and slot stories into STAR.
    *   Sound real, so reviewers see a genuine person.
*   **Don't:**
    *   Stick notes beside the camera or recite a pre-written script.
    *   Talk too fast without breathing pauses, or fill gaps with "um/ah/like."
    *   Sound stiff and academic, losing the natural feel of speech.

### Decisions and revisions

*   **Do:**
    *   Keep a **single source of truth** and follow one consistent strategy and professional feedback.
    *   Make sure your essays and videos present one consistent picture and set of values.
*   **Don't:**
    *   Take conflicting edits from many people (parents, teachers, Reddit, friends).
    *   Revise blindly to every opinion (seven different versions), losing your identity and core logic (lost cohesion/voice).

## Part 5: Timeline, Final Polish and Mindset

### Timeline: starting in October vs. scrambling in January/February

```
Two paths:
[Start in October]   ──► find your core stories ──► build your experience bank ──► 7-day simulator loop ──► submit with confidence, get the offer
[Rush in Jan/Feb]    ──► deadline close ──► all-night drafting and memorising ──► stiff, half-hearted videos ──► rejection
```

*   **Starting in October:** time to research Rotman's courses and faculty, build your experience bank properly, practise repeatedly in a simulator and polish your essays over several rounds, calmly.
*   **Rushing in January/February:** scrambling near the deadline, writing essays and memorising scripts overnight, stiff video performances, lowering your standards to "just get it done" — and getting cut.

### Final polishing standards

1.  **Read out loud:** read your essays aloud. If they feel clunky, slow or long-winded, reviewers will feel the same.
2.  **Elevate action verbs:** replace flat words with strong ones (*Led*, *Initiated*, *Transformed*, *Rebuilt*, *Spearheaded*).
3.  **Align with Rotman's values:** make sure your story reflects the competencies Rotman looks for (business sense, innovation, collaboration, resilience).
4.  **Cut the fluff:** delete every optional modifier so every word earns its place.
5.  **⚠️ An AI proofreading warning:** relying too much on AI tools (heavy Grammarly or ChatGPT polishing) will **flatten your natural voice**, making your writing cold and mechanical and losing the authenticity reviewers care about.

### The 7-day interview simulator protocol

To reach 4–5 on Kira Talent, run a seven-day practice loop:

```
Seven-day simulator path:
Days 1–2: baseline ──► record 5 raw answers ──► find filler and pace problems (score 1–2)
Days 3–4: structure ──► tighten STAR and turning points ──► improve eye contact (score 3)
Days 5–6: competencies ──► add deep reflection and mindset change ──► tune tone and energy (score 4)
Day 7:    pressure test ──► random blind questions ──► natural, sincere, structured delivery (score 4–5)
```

*   **Volume and tracking:** at least **20** practice recordings in seven days.
*   **Metrics to watch:**
    *   *Vocal clarity:* clear pronunciation and a steady, strong tone.
    *   *Filler word frequency:* "um/ah/like" no more than twice a minute.
    *   *Time utilisation:* finish a 60-second answer in 52–58 seconds, without stopping early or being cut off.
    *   *Transition smoothness:* move naturally from situation (S) to action (A) to reflection (R), with no sign of a script.

### Managing your mindset

The supplementary season (September to February) overlaps heavily with school exams.
*   **SMART goals and Pomodoro:** break the work into small units ("today I'll only revise the hook of my activity essay").
*   **Productive bursts:** work in focused 25-minute blocks and stop procrastinating.
*   **Reduce anxiety through preparation:** the best cure for anxiety is thorough preparation. A solid experience bank and real practice turn uncertainty into control, so you can submit a supplementary application that truly represents your best.

## FAQ

### What's in the Rotman Commerce supplementary application?

Four parts: a 200-word known activity essay, a 60-second known video, a 10-minute 200-word random essay and a random Kira video interview.

### How much prep time do random video questions give?

Usually about 90 seconds to think and 60 to answer. Use STAR: situation 10s, task 5s, action 30s, result and takeaway 15s.

### Can I script the known 60-second video?

Plan the structure, but don't read it. Reviewers who detect a script place you in the lowest band.

### What activities does Rotman value?

Deep projects that use business analysis or technical tools and show measurable results, like an Excel forecast and social A/B tests that lifted event conversion by 35%.
