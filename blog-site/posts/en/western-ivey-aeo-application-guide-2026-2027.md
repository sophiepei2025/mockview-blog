> **Short answer:** Ivey AEO is decided 50% on academics and 50% on the supplementary application. A 90%+ average is the threshold; what separates applicants is two 500-word leadership essays, 5 supporting activities and 15 awards, five Kira video questions, and references who can verify the details you wrote.

**Key points**

- Process: OUAC → Western Student ID and Ivey Portal → references → essays and activities → Kira → submit.
- Suggested essay split: hook 10% / context 15% / turning point 15% / action 30% / result 15% / reflection 15%.
- References must be able to confirm specific actions and numbers; brief them 6–8 weeks ahead.
- After AEO, keep an 80%+ average for two years (78%+ in engineering and similar programs).
- Common Kira themes: setbacks and conflict, team leadership, business ethics, role models, Why Ivey.

### 1.2 Timeline and pacing

When you start determines how deep your essays go and how well your referees can support you. Compare starting early (October) with cramming late (January/February):

| | Early start (October) | Late cram (January/February) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Essay depth** | Time to refine the story, cut the excess and reach "Level 5" reflection. | Late-night rewrites before the deadline produce a list of experiences and shallow clichés. |
| **Referees** | Referees confirmed 6–8 weeks ahead with essay details shared, so they can vouch with confidence. | Referees found 1–2 weeks before the deadline don't know the details and may respond late. |
| **Kira performance** | 7–14 days of timed simulator practice builds comfort on camera and a natural rhythm. | No practice: freezing on camera and obvious scripting lead to low scores. |
| **Mindset and quality** | Calm and iterative, with a clearly authentic voice. | Under time pressure, "excellence" becomes "submit before the deadline," and the essays lose authenticity. |

#### Key tasks from October to January

*   **October–November: discovery, structure and referees**
    *   **Find your X-factor:** review your high school experiences and use the T-model to choose the two projects you've invested in most (material for the two 500-word essays).
    *   **Lock in referees:** contact supervisors or teachers who can confirm your actions and numbers, and share your first draft and key data with them.
    *   **Shortlist the supplementary items:** choose five supporting activities and up to 15 meaningful awards to show breadth.
*   **December: drafting, focused feedback and Kira prep**
    *   **Finish the first drafts:** apply the six-part structure and push reflection up to "Level 5."
    *   **Use a single source of truth:** take detailed feedback from one experienced mentor only. Don't ask parents, classmates and Reddit for conflicting opinions; that flattens your voice (essay flattening).
    *   **Kira practice:** record daily on a Kira simulator, working through the five core question types for seven straight days to reach the 4–5 range.
*   **January: proofreading, read-aloud test and submission**
    *   **Read out loud:** read your essays aloud to cut awkward phrasing and fluff so the language flows naturally.
    *   **Track referees:** check in the Ivey Portal that referees have confirmed through the official email.
    *   **Submit early:** submit at least 3–5 days before the official deadline (usually late January or early February) to avoid system outages.

## 2. The 50/50 Rule: Academics and Leadership

Western Ivey AEO is assessed on a strict **50% academic grades + 50% supplementary application (leadership and overall profile)** basis.

**Western Ivey AEO assessment**
2. Academics          |               |  Leadership / supp app
3. 50%            |               |          50%


### 2.1 The academic 50%: thresholds and tiers

*   **Grade inflation and where you really stand:** high school grades are inflated, and 90%+, 95% or even 98%+ averages are common. In Ivey AEO's assessment, a high average is simply **a box to tick**. A 97 and a 95 make no real difference to admissions officers; grades alone can't secure an offer.
*   **The supplementary application decides it:** when thousands of applicants arrive with 92%+ or 95%+, the offer depends entirely on the other 50% — essay depth, quantified activity results and how authentically you think in the Kira interview.
*   **Keeping AEO status and your university habits:** AEO status is only the start. During your first two years in your Western program (BMOS, Engineering, Science and so on) you must:
    1.  complete the required high school courses;
    2.  keep a cumulative average of **80%+** over the first two years (78%+ for demanding programs such as Engineering);
    3.  continue taking part in extracurricular leadership.
    The time management, resilience and leadership habits you build in high school are what keep you at 80%+ through heavy coursework and club life and get you into Ivey HBA in third year.

---

### 2.2 The leadership 50%: the T-model and core traits

When Ivey assesses leadership, they're looking for people who will thrive in Ivey's signature **case-method** classroom:

*   **Initiative:** not passive participation. Spotting bottlenecks, challenging the status quo and building new platforms or processes without being told.
*   **Resilience and adaptability:** adjusting quickly in a crisis, with scarce resources or after a failed plan, and making decisions amid ambiguity.
*   **Collaboration and active listening:** leadership isn't giving orders; it's building trust, listening to different perspectives and bringing people from different backgrounds to agreement.
*   **Impact:** projects that make measurable change and leave a lasting legacy for a community, school or organization.

#### Strategy for non-traditional leaders

**Key point: leadership is not the same as a title.** Admissions officers value leadership in non-traditional settings. Even if you aren't student council president or a club head, showing ownership and problem-solving in informal settings is often worth more than an inflated title:

*   *Example 1 (balancing family and work):* during senior year, working as a part-time evening supervisor at the family restaurant or business, redesigning inventory and checkout processes to raise efficiency by 15% while keeping up with demanding coursework.
*   *Example 2 (stepping up in a crisis without a title):* when a community event was short-staffed after the organizer left, taking charge of volunteer roles and coordinating resources so the event ran smoothly.

#### Building the T-model into your application

```
                    Breadth: 5 supporting activities / 15 awards
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │   Clubs    │   Sports   │  Volunteer │ Jobs/projects │ Arts/other │
        └────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     │ Depth: 1–2 projects you led over time
                                     │ (the two 500-word leadership essays)
                                     │
                                     ▼
```

*   **Breadth:** the five supporting activities and 15 awards show a range of interests (sports, work, community service) and a well-rounded picture.
*   **Depth:** 1–2 long-term projects you led and invested heavily in (the material for your two essays) show leadership depth, breakthroughs on hard problems and quantified impact.

## 3. The Two 500-Word Core Leadership Essays

### 3.1 Choosing a story and structuring it

Drop "Since I was young…" and "I have always been passionate about business." Openings like these tire reviewers. Start with a specific dramatic moment or core conflict.

#### The six-part, 500-word split

To keep the essay tight and focused, allocate the 500 words like this:

**1. Hook                        [~10% | 50 words]**
2. • A moment of conflict, a dramatic failure or a surprising fact.
3. 2. Context (the 5 Ws)          [~15% | 75 words]
4. • When, where, your role and the resource constraints.
5. 3. Catalyst                    [~15% | 75 words]
6. • The core crisis, team disagreement or breakdown.
7. 4. Core action                 [~30% | 150 words] (the heart of it)
8. • Your decisions, how you mobilized people and the path you took.
9. 5. Outcome                     [~15% | 75 words]
10. • Precise data showing your actions worked and the project's scale.
11. 6. Takeaway                    [~15% | 75 words]
12. • How your thinking evolved and how it reshaped your leadership.


#### Example hooks

*   *Example A (crisis):* "When the sensor circuit we were testing shorted, sparked a small fire and set off the basement sprinklers, soaking all our equipment, we had less than 48 hours until the provincial science fair deadline."
*   *Example B (social impact and innovation):* "When eight-year-old Lucas first gripped a cup with the prosthetic hand we'd built from 3D-printed parts and recycled cable, six months of engineering setbacks and a dozen collapsed models finally turned into real change."

---

### 3.2 Action verbs, quantified results and deep reflection

Follow "show, don't tell" throughout.

#### Upgrading your phrasing with numbers

*   *Flat:* "I volunteered at an animal shelter, helped look after the animals and showed great leadership."
*   *High-impact:* "Rebuilt the shelter's adoption matching process with a multi-factor assessment form, placing 50 rescue animals in long-term homes."
*   *Flat:* "I organized a fundraiser and contributed to my community."
*   *High-impact:* "Launched and coordinated 25 volunteers, set up an online auction and secured sponsorship from 10 local businesses, raising $12,000 and providing 1,000 meals to low-income families."

#### High-impact action verbs

*   **Spearheaded** | **Transformed**
*   **Rebuilt** | **Engineered**
*   **Mobilized** | **Initiated**

#### Raising reflection from Level 1 to Level 5

Self-awareness is what separates top essays from ordinary ones.

| Level | Type | Example | How admissions reads it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1** | Superficial | "Through this experience I learned that teamwork and communication are very important, and I became more hard-working." | **Loses points.** Generic, with no personal depth; anyone could write it. |
| **Level 5** | Mindset shift | "I realized I had mistaken leadership for control and giving orders. When the project hit a deadlock, I changed my approach — from issuing instructions to building trust — and set up a way for everyone to speak safely, which unlocked the team's own ideas." | **Scores high.** Strong self-analysis, maturity and a real evolution in how you understand leadership. |

---

### 3.3 Referee verification and the single source of truth

Ivey verifies essay details rigorously through referees:

*   **Who qualifies:** a high school teacher, school official, club supervisor or job manager who personally saw the actions, decisions and results in your essays. **Never family, friends, classmates or someone you've hired with no real connection.**
*   **Brief them early:** before submitting, talk to your referees and share the final essays and key numbers (amount raised, project scale, dates) so their response to Ivey's verification email matches exactly.

#### Avoiding essay flattening from too many editors

A common fatal mistake at the editing stage is sending your essays to seven or eight people: parents, teachers, classmates, Reddit users, relatives.
*   **A real failure:** a strong applicant rewrote her essays seven times to satisfy seven reviewers. Parents wanted more modesty, a teacher wanted academic formality, friends wanted trendy phrases. The essay lost its authentic voice, became flat, contradictory and empty, and was rejected.
*   **Best practice:** **use a single source of truth.** Choose one experienced expert or alumni mentor who really understands Ivey AEO admissions as your only source of detailed feedback, and keep your story clear and your voice your own.

## 4. Five Supplementary Activities and 15 Awards

### 4.1 Choosing and describing your five supplementary activities

The five supplementary activities show the **breadth** of your T-model and should cover areas like clubs, sports, community service, part-time work, arts or personal projects.

#### Formula: strong verb + core responsibility + quantified result

Each description has very little space, so make every word count:

*   *Example 1 (sports):* "Captained the school volleyball team, ran eight hours of tactical training a week, coordinated 12 players' offence and defence, and led the team to third place at provincials."
*   *Example 2 (part-time / non-traditional leadership):* "Worked as an evening supervisor at the family restaurant while balancing senior year, streamlining inventory and checkout to cut waste by 15%."
*   *Example 3 (passion project):* "Founded a technology-help program for seniors, recruiting 25 volunteers to run weekly device lessons for 100+ residents at two care centres."

**Complement, don't repeat:** supplementary activities should complement the two core essays, not repeat the same experiences, so your application shows a full, three-dimensional picture.

---

### 4.2 Ranking and categorising up to 15 awards

When listing up to 15 awards, prioritise like this:

1.  **Level:** international/national > provincial/city > school/community.
2.  **Variety:** academic honours (such as honour roll), leadership recognition and competitions (business, math or computing contests).
3.  **Quality over quantity:** if you don't have 15 strong awards, don't pad with participation awards. Five to eight well-recognised awards that show real pursuit of excellence are more convincing than a long, meaningless list.

## 5. The Kira Video Interview (Five Questions)

The Kira interview is where you show real communication, quick thinking and depth.

### 5.1 How Kira is scored: mechanics and the 1–5 rubric

#### 30,000+ human reviewers and what AI actually does

*   **Video is 100% human-scored:** Kira video answers are **scored entirely by trained human reviewers**, not algorithms or AI. Kira has a network of more than 30,000 reviewers across disciplines, each calibrated to Ivey's competency model.
*   **What AI does:** AI on Kira **only handles grammar, spelling and technical checks on written questions**; it plays no part in scoring video.
*   **Reviewer fatigue and pattern recognition:** in one admissions season a reviewer may watch dozens or hundreds of videos. After 40 videos, 30-odd of which start with "I am passionate about business…", fatigue sets in. **Specific detail and an unexpected opening (contrast and specificity) are what make an answer memorable and earn high marks.**
*   **Summed scoring:** Kira uses **total sums scoring**. Competency scores are added up, so a serious failure on one competency drags the total down. One great answer can't hide another that collapsed; every question has to be solid.

#### The 1–5 rubric

| Score | Rubric description | What it looks like to a reviewer |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Vague or superficial example; little awareness of own contribution; no meaningful reflection or growth. | "I sometimes help my team, and I learned leadership." (No detail, no ownership, an empty story.) |
| **2** | Limited detail; doesn't directly answer the question; some ability to listen to others' perspectives; minimal reflection. | Describes a basic process but stays on the surface and misses the competency. |
| **3** | Clear, relevant example; reasonable adaptability and reflection; some insight into lessons and growth. | Clearly explains organizing an activity or resolving a conflict; competent, in the safe zone. |
| **4** | Thoughtful, specific example; strong initiative, adaptability and respect for others' perspectives; deep reflection on lessons and changed behaviour. | Detailed; explains how you planned, empowered the team and upgraded your leadership. Clear ownership. |
| **5** | Vivid, authentic, highly reflective; maturity, self-direction, creativity or composure under pressure; handles a complex setback clearly; analyses the mindset shift and future behaviour change. | Compelling and real; candid about failure or complexity, analyses how your thinking evolved, and shows exceptional leadership maturity and character. |

---

### 5.2 STAR+T answer logic

In a 1–2 minute answer, **STAR+T** keeps the logic tight and layered:

```
S (Situation)  ─► Background and context fast (10–15 seconds)
T (Task)       ─► The responsibility, bottleneck or goal (10–15 seconds)
A (Action)     ─► The specific leadership actions and your decisions (40–50 seconds) [core]
R (Result)     ─► Quantified outcomes and impact on the team (15–20 seconds) [key to a high score]
T (Takeaway)   ─► Deep reflection, mindset shift and future application (15–20 seconds) [key to a high score]
```

*   **Why Result and Takeaway are the keys to a high score:** most 1–2 answers stop at S and A (what you did). A 4–5 answer must prove its actions worked with **R (quantified results)** and show mature self-awareness with **T (takeaway)**.

---

### 5.3 Five classic and likely question types

#### Type 1: overcoming setbacks, conflict or sudden change
*   **Approach:** show resilience and emotional control; never blame others.
*   **Structure:** the sudden conflict or difficulty ➔ a three-step response (understand the bottleneck ➔ gather perspectives ➔ carry out a shared solution) ➔ result and reflection.
*   **Highlight:** "When budget cuts threatened the project, I didn't push people into overtime. I held a meeting to hear everyone's concerns and turned separate events into one joint gala, making a crisis into an opportunity."

#### Type 2: team leadership — micromanaging vs. empowering
*   **Approach:** show the shift from **micromanagement** to **empowerment**.
*   **Structure:** team running inefficiently ➔ the root cause (too much control sapped motivation) ➔ a change in approach (delegating and building trust) ➔ results and what you learned.
*   **Highlight:** explain clearly how you let go of control, trusted your teammates and got better execution and new ideas as a result.

#### Type 3: ethics, ESG and decisions (conscientious consumerism)
*   **Approach:** tests business ethics and social responsibility.
*   **Case reference:** describe your experience as a conscientious consumer. For example, a friend discovered a fast-fashion site had copied her artwork without paying her. Use that to explain why you buy art merchandise directly from independent artists and pay attention to ethical supply chains.
*   **Key message:** "In business decisions and everyday spending, we're responsible not only for numbers and profit but for values. Protecting intellectual property and ethical supply chains is the foundation of lasting brand loyalty."

#### Type 4: personal achievement or a role model
*   **Approach:** don't just praise wealth; explain how your role model balances business success and social contribution (philanthropy).
*   **Case reference:** Bill Gates.
    *   *Business:* co-founded Microsoft and built it into a global giant.
    *   *Philanthropy and social impact:* after stepping down as CEO he focused on the Gates Foundation's work to eliminate disease. For example, he paid **US$76 million** to clear Nigeria's polio-vaccine debt to Japan, helping Nigeria move toward becoming polio-free.
*   **Key message:** "To me, great leadership isn't only huge business success; it's using the resources that success creates to solve the world's most serious social and health crises."

#### Type 5: why Western Ivey AEO (program fit)
*   **Approach:** no generic lines about rankings or a beautiful campus. Show strong fit based on **deep research**.
*   **Structure:** your long-term goal ➔ Ivey's **case method**, specific labs or modules ➔ how those resources support your growth.
*   **Highlight:** explain that you want to debate ideas with talented peers from different backgrounds in Ivey's intense case discussions and build the ability to make sound business decisions with incomplete information.

---

### 5.4 Delivery, energy and unscripted readiness

#### The fatal mistake: how scripted answers give themselves away

In the Kira interview, **reciting a prepared script is a fatal mistake.** Reviewers can tell within seconds from these audio and visual signals:

**Scripted / read vs. natural, authentic answers**

| 1. Voice dynamics | • Tone goes flat and mechanical. |
| --- | --- |
| 2. Eye contact | • Eyes scan left and right (reading<br>the screen or notes). |
| 3. Pacing and pauses | • Fast, even pace with pauses in<br>unnatural places. |


**What reviewers actually say:** "The whole reason we use Kira is to see the real student. When we get scripted responses it's really disappointing."

#### What scores well, and the seven-day practice method

1.  **Lens contact:** look straight into the camera lens, not at yourself on screen, to create a sense of conversation with the reviewer.
2.  **Pauses and composure:** on a hard question, a steady 1–2 second pause shows **composure** under pressure; rushing shows nerves.
3.  **Seven-day practice:** record daily on a Kira-style video simulator for seven days:

```
Day 1–2 ➔ Get used to the countdown and the camera (score: 1–2)
Day 3–4 ➔ Learn STAR+T, improve transitions and cut filler words (score: 2–3)
Day 5–6 ➔ Add specific detail and deep takeaways to the five question types (score: 3–4)
Day 7   ➔ Answer random questions naturally and confidently (score: 4–5)
```

## 6. Western Ivey AEO Dos & Don'ts

### 6.1 Dos

*   **Start early:** begin discovery and essay planning in October to leave time for several drafts and Kira practice. Don't leave it to January.
*   **Keep a single source of truth:** one trusted, experienced mentor as your only feedback source protects your authentic voice.
*   **Read out loud:** read your essays aloud to catch awkward sentences, long-winded phrasing and logic gaps (cut the fluff).
*   **Build a strong cause-and-effect story:** give your application a clear through-line connecting past breakthroughs, core values and how your leadership is developing.
*   **Use STAR+T:** in essays and video, deliver quantified results and a real takeaway that shows self-awareness beyond your peers.
*   **Show non-traditional leadership:** leadership isn't a title. Stepping up at a job, a family business or in a crisis carries real weight.
*   **Brief referees early:** explain essay details and key numbers to referees 6–8 weeks ahead so they can verify quickly and accurately when Ivey emails them.

---

### 6.2 Don'ts

*   **Don't stack buzzwords:** avoid "I am passionate" and "I have strong communication skills"; admissions officers tire of them quickly.
*   **Don't let AI flatten your voice:** tools can fix spelling, but don't let AI rewrite your essays; it erases your personality and warmth.
*   **Don't ask too many people:** passing your essays among parents, classmates, online forums and relatives leads to conflicting edits and essay flattening.
*   **Don't contact referees at the last minute:** finding referees just before the January deadline risks late responses that can void your application.
*   **Don't use stale openings:** drop "Since I was young" and "I have always loved business"; open with a specific conflict or surprising fact.
*   **Don't use a script in the Kira interview:** reciting or reading from the screen makes your tone flat and gets you marked "scripted," dropping you to a 1–2.
*   **Don't chase quantity over depth:** avoid listing unrelated, shallow activities; follow the T-model and put depth before breadth.

## FAQ

### Does Ivey AEO care more about grades or the supplementary application?

They are weighted equally. A 90%+ average is the threshold, and a 95 versus a 97 makes no real difference; the essays, activities and Kira interview decide it.

### What do I need to keep AEO status?

A cumulative average of 80%+ (78%+ in programs like engineering) over your first two years, plus continued extracurricular leadership, to enter Ivey HBA in year three.

### Can a relative or friend be my reference?

No. References should be teachers, school staff, club supervisors or job managers who saw the actions and results you describe.

### What if I don't have 15 awards?

Don't pad with participation awards. Five to eight meaningful awards are more convincing than a full list.
