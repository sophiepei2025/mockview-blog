> **Short answer:** Each Kira Talent video answer is blind-scored 1–5 by two or three human reviewers calibrated to the school's rubric, then combined; AI only checks written answers. Universities look most for four abilities: clear communication, composure under pressure, leadership through ownership, and honest, deep reflection.

**Key points**

- Flow: submit → routed by program → 2–3 blind reviewers → weighted combination and arbitration → admissions committee.
- Since 2024, summed scoring and dual calculation mean a single 1 is very hard to offset.
- A 3 is safe but forgettable; a 4–5 needs ownership and changed behaviour.
- STAR+T: situation 10% / task 10% / action 45% / result 15% / reflection 20%.
- Two-minute prep: 20s to pick a story → 30s to structure → 20s for reflection → 50s to rehearse.

## 1. How Kira Talent Reviews and Scores Your Answers

### 1.1 What AI does and what human reviewers do

At top schools (Toronto Rotman and Engineering, Waterloo CS/Engineering/AFM, McMaster Health Sciences and Engineering, UBC Sauder, Queen's Commerce and Health Sciences, Western Ivey and others), many applicants believe Kira Talent uses AI neural networks to score your facial expressions, micro-expressions, eye contact or keywords automatically. That misunderstanding often leads to mechanical, performed answers that cross the reviewers' red lines.

In fact, Kira Talent draws a clear line between what AI does and what **human reviewers** do:

*   **What AI does:** it only handles the **technical side** of written responses. Algorithms scan spelling, grammar, punctuation and basic text structure to support compliance checks and flag possible cheating. AI plays no part in assessing video responses or your human qualities.
*   **What human reviewers do:** every video response is watched, analysed and scored independently by **calibrated** human reviewers. They judge your reasoning, how you handle pressure, your initiative as a leader and the depth of your reflection to decide whether you have real potential for the program.

| AI checks (written responses) | Human reviewers (video responses) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **[Where]** All written responses | **[Where]** All video responses |
| **[Technical text checks]** Grammar, spelling and punctuation. | **[Communication and presence]** Vocal clarity, structure, body language, eye contact with the lens and energy. |
| **[Structure and integrity checks]** Basic text structure, plus spotting unusual patterns from pasted scripts or AI-written text. | **[Ability and depth]** Ownership, composure, reflection and genuine maturity. |
| **[Authority]** Technical flags and reference only; no say in competency scores. | **[Authority]** Independent 1–5 scores with written comments that decide each competency score. |

---

### 1.2 30,000+ reviewers: how your video is routed and scored

Kira Talent has a global network of **more than 30,000 calibrated professional reviewers**. They aren't assigned at random; they're matched and trained on the competency model and rubric of the specific program (UofT, Waterloo, Queen's, Western Ivey and so on). For example, a reviewer assessing UofT Engineering videos has fully absorbed that faculty's weighting and benchmarks for "engineering thinking and teamwork."

After you submit, your video moves through a closed loop that combines automation and human review:

```text
[ Step 1: submission and preprocessing ]
You finish the Kira interview; the system standardises the audio and video, and AI runs grammar and technical checks on written responses.
                   │
                   ▼
[ Step 2: program matching and blind assignment ]
Based on the program's competency model (UofT, Waterloo, Queen's, Ivey…), the video goes at random to 2–3 independent reviewers calibrated on that program's rubric.
                   │
                   ▼
[ Step 3: blind scoring by several reviewers ]
Reviewers work independently (double-blind), watching the video and scoring each competency on the 1–5 scale with comments.
                   │
                   ▼
[ Step 4: weighting and checks ]
The system calculates a weighted aggregate; if reviewers disagree widely, a senior reviewer arbitrates.
                   │
                   ▼
[ Step 5: sent to the admissions committee ]
The final competency report, scores and comments go to the university's admissions committee as a core input to the decision.
```

### 1.3 Pattern recognition and reviewer psychology

In one admissions cycle, a calibrated reviewer watches hundreds of answers under heavy time pressure. Under that physical and mental fatigue, the brain develops a sharp, unconscious **pattern recognition**:

*   **Fatigue and clichés (red flags):** when a reviewer watches 40 videos and 30 open with "I've always been passionate about business..." or "Since I was young...", they put up a mental wall and file the applicant as interchangeable. In Kira, being forgettable is far more damaging than a stumble or talking slightly fast.
*   **The power of contrast:** when video 31 breaks the pattern and opens on a specific crisis, a real setback in thinking or an unexpected scene, the **contrast effect** wakes the reviewer up. Admissions remembers applicants who show real drama and a turning point in their thinking.
*   **The scripting penalty:** many applicants try to recite a perfect pre-written script. The moment you recite on camera, your voice flattens, your eyes drift slightly and your pace turns mechanical. These signs trigger the reviewer's disappointment and a low score.

## 2. The Official 1–5 Rubric and How Scores Are Calculated

### 2.1 The 1–5 scale

Kira Talent and its partner universities generally use a 1–5 competency rubric. The key question is whether your answer stops at **participation** or shows real **ownership** and **reflection**.

| Score | Level | Official criteria and behaviour | Typical example |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Vague, no reflection | A vague, shallow or generic example; little awareness of your own contribution; no meaningful reflection or learning; may sound recited. | *"I sometimes help my team, and I learned leadership from it."* (No context, no personal action, no real change.) |
| **2** | Limited detail | Limited detail or doesn't answer the core question; shows little empathy or other perspectives; minimal reflection with no link to growth. | Describes a team experience briefly but not the bottleneck or how it was solved; reflection stops at "teamwork is important." |
| **3** | Clear but shallow<br>**(safe territory)** | A clear, relevant example; reasonable adaptability and problem solving; some insight into what you learned and how you grew. **(Safe, but hard to stand out.)** | *"I led a team presentation and calmed things down when teammates disagreed."* (Basic competence, but no account of the conflict's nature, your specific intervention or how you changed.) |
| **4** | Specific and reflective<br>**(shows initiative)** | A detailed, specific example; strong **ownership, not just participation**, adaptability and respect for diverse views; deep reflection on how it reshaped future thinking and behaviour. | *"When I noticed the team falling behind, I replanned our workflow. I learned to motivate the team by listening instead of micromanaging."* (Initiative on a bottleneck and a clear change in behaviour.) |
| **5** | Real and deep<br>**(maturity and behaviour change)** | A vivid, authentic, reflective example with composure under pressure; high maturity and self-direction; handles setbacks clearly and with purpose; turns a past failure into fundamental behaviour change. | *"When our project collapsed over a budget conflict, I realised I'd focused on control rather than real collaboration. I changed how I led, set up transparent communication, and our next project succeeded. That experience completely changed how I lead teams today."* |

### 2.2 The scoring update: summed scoring and dual calculation

In 2024 Kira Talent updated how scores are combined, adding stricter rules against gaming and imbalance:

1.  **Competency weighting:** each program sets its own weights. Rotman Commerce might weight communication more heavily; Waterloo Computer Science might weight logical thinking and composure.
2.  **Total sums scoring and dual calculation:** the old model **averaged** competency scores; the new one uses **total sums scoring** with **dual calculation**, adding scores up across every dimension.
3.  **The cumulative drag of an extreme low:** under summed scoring, one extremely low score (a 1 on a question because you froze or went off-topic) creates an **unrecoverable cumulative drag** on your total. You can't rely on one brilliant answer (a 5) to make up for a disastrous one (a 1). Staying steady at 4+ on every question is the math of getting an offer.

## 3. The Four Abilities Top Schools Really Look For

### 3.1 Communication and presentation

In a video interview, delivery and content each carry about half the weight:
*   **Eye contact:** fix your gaze on the **physical camera lens**, not the on-screen image or notes, to build a real visual connection.
*   **Energy and tone:** keep a positive, confident vocal energy; avoid flat, mechanical delivery.
*   **Gestures and posture:** use natural gestures to support your logic, with relaxed shoulders and an open expression.
*   **A one-second pause instead of fillers:** avoid "um," "ah," "like" and "you know." To a reviewer, **a deliberate one-second pause shows composure**, while frequent fillers reveal nerves.

### 3.2 Stress management and adaptability

Kira uses a timed format with **no redos** (for example, 2 minutes to prepare and 2 to answer).
*   **What it's really testing:** admissions isn't checking your memory; it's answering one question: "Can this student think clearly and stay calm in a high-pressure, uncertain situation?"
*   **How to adapt:** when you get an unfamiliar question, breathe steadily, use the prep time to sketch a structure fast and show **composure under pressure**.

### 3.3 Leadership and ownership

Top schools' definition of leadership has moved well beyond titles (club president, captain, student council head):
*   **Ownership vs. micromanaging:** the leadership they value is taking **ownership** and driving a solution when there's no title, few resources or a deadlock.
*   **A real example:** Liam, a high school student whose father has multiple sclerosis (MS), started a national charity challenge called *"Murph for MS"* (a gruelling CrossFit workout every day for 31 days). With no corporate or school title behind him, Liam reached out to more than 200 gyms across Canada, worked through many rejections and coordination problems, and raised over $20,000 for research. Taking initiative and pulling resources together in adversity, and closing the translation gap, is exactly what a 5 on Kira's rubric looks like.

```text
[Low-score logic vs. high-score logic]
❌ 1–2 (micromanaging and titles):
"I'm student council president. I assigned everyone tasks and made sure they finished on time."

4–5 (real ownership and driving collaboration):
"When the project stalled after budget cuts, I didn't push a top-down order. I brought the stakeholders together, merged each group's separate budget into one fund and broke the coordination deadlock."
```

### 3.4 Authenticity and reflection

Reviewers agree: "The whole reason we use Kira is to see the real student."
*   **The damage scripts do:** reciting makes your voice flatten and your eyes wander, triggering the scripting penalty.
*   **Why reflection carries so much weight:** what separates a 3 (safe) from a 4/5 is **subterranean self-exploration**. Your answer must show a **mindset change** and a **behaviour change** — how the experience reshaped how you think and act today.

## 4. A High-Scoring Framework and Your Experience Bank

### 4.1 Using the STAR+T model (Situation, Task, Action, Result + Takeaway)

In a two-minute answer, the **STAR+T model** gives you both structure and depth:

```text
[ Situation: 10% ] ──> [ Task: 10% ] ──> [ Action: 45% ] ──> [ Result: 15% ] ──> [ Takeaway: 20% ]
  Background fast        Core bottleneck     Your specific actions   Quantified result    Mindset and behaviour change
```

*   **Situation (10%):** one or two sentences on time, place and context.
*   **Task (10%):** the core bottleneck to solve or goal to reach.
*   **Action (45%, the core):** **the three specific steps you took**, why you took them and how you overcame resistance and conflict.
*   **Result (15%):** concrete numbers or tangible impact.
*   **Takeaway / Reflection (20%, the key to a high score):** your mindset change and behaviour change.

#### A model answer (team budget conflict and leadership):

> **[Situation - 10%]** In 11th grade, I served on our student council's athletic committee, which managed event funding for all school sports teams. Due to rising inflation, the traditional $200 allocated per team for end-of-year celebrations was no longer viable to host individual events.
>
> **[Task - 10%]** As a committee member, my goal was to resolve the budget deficit while ensuring every sports team felt equally recognized and celebrated without causing inter-team friction.
>
> **[Action - 45%]** I approached this bottleneck in three structured steps. First, I held an open forum with all team captains to actively listen to their individual perspectives rather than imposing a top-down decision. Second, after realizing everyone prioritized event quality over team isolation, I proposed a unified solution: consolidating our individual $200 allowances into a single $2,000 aggregate budget to host a joint student-athlete banquet. Third, I managed the cross-team logistics, empowering captains to lead specific sub-committees for music, food, and awards.
>
> **[Result - 15%]** The joint banquet was a massive success, bringing together over 100 student-athletes, raising team satisfaction scores by 35%, and staying strictly within our budgetary constraints.
>
> **[Takeaway/Reflection - 20%]** This experience fundamentally shifted my perspective on leadership. I realized that effective problem-solving requires actively engaging diverse stakeholders first, rather than rushing into compromised micromanagement. It taught me to build consensus through collaboration, a mindset change that now dictates how I resolve group conflicts today.

```text
[Why this answer scores 4.5–5.0]
1. Precise timing: Action takes almost half the answer, broken into listen–consolidate–empower, showing strong ownership.
2. No buzzwords: no "I am a strong leader"; leadership is proven through specific coordinating actions.
3. Real reflection: the takeaway names a mindset change (avoiding micromanagement) and a behaviour change (building consensus), fully meeting the 5-point standard.
```

### 4.2 Your experience bank and the two-minute prep plan

Questions are random, so don't try to guess them. Before the interview, use **subterranean self-exploration** to build an **experience bank of 8–10 real experiences**.

#### Eight scenarios your bank must cover:
1.  **Handling conflict or disagreement**
2.  **Overcoming a major failure or setback**
3.  **Receiving difficult feedback and adjusting**
4.  **Taking initiative and leading without a title**
5.  **Problem-solving under pressure and constraints**
6.  **Adapting to unexpected change**
7.  **Taking responsibility for a mistake**
8.  **A moment that fundamentally changed your perspective**

#### How to use 2 minutes (120 seconds) of prep:

```text
⏱️ 0:00 - 0:20 (20s) [Identify the competency and pick a story]
  └─ Work out what the question is testing (e.g. adaptability or leadership).
  └─ Pull the single best-fitting real story from your experience bank.

⏱️ 0:20 - 0:50 (30s) [Outline STAR+T]
  └─ One sentence each for Situation and Task.
  └─ Three specific Action steps (communicate, decide, execute).

⏱️ 0:50 - 1:10 (20s) [Lock in the takeaway]
  └─ Your key line: mindset change + behaviour change.

⏱️ 1:10 - 2:00 (50s) [Rehearse silently and get ready on camera]
  └─ Run through it silently, steady your breathing, eyes on the lens, ready to record.
```

## 5. Kira Talent Dos & Don'ts

### 5.1 Dos

*   **Practise under real, measurable conditions:** rehearse with a real camera and countdown. Use a professional tool such as Edmoat's MockView, and check yourself on these four metrics:
    1.  **Vocal clarity**
    2.  **Filler word frequency**
    3.  **Time utilisation ratio (aim for 85%–95%)**
    4.  **Structure and pacing**
*   **Control your pace and pause strategically:** slow down a little (130–150 words per minute) and speak clearly; when you need to think, use a natural one-second pause instead of "um" or "ah" to show composure.
*   **Back up claims with numbers and actions:** give clear figures in your Result ("raised $20,000," "30% more efficient") to remove any sense of vagueness.
*   **Keep your eyes on the lens:** look at the camera lens, not the screen or notes on your desk, so reviewers feel a direct, real connection.
*   **Optimise your room and equipment:** a quiet room with a clean background, light on your face from the front (not behind), a good microphone and a stable connection.

### 5.2 Don'ts

*   **Don't recite a pre-written script:** never read or recite. It flattens your voice and makes your eyes wander, triggering the **scripting penalty** and being judged "disappointing."
*   **Don't pile on clichés and buzzwords:** avoid unsupported lines like "I am a natural leader," "I have passionate communication skills" or "I always give 110%."
*   **Don't speak in generalities without an example:** don't stay at abstract principles ("teamwork matters because many hands make light work"); go straight to a specific personal experience.
*   **Don't run out of time before your reflection:** watch the countdown; don't spend too long on the Situation, or your Action and the all-important Takeaway get cut off.

## FAQ

### How many people watch each Kira video?

Two or three independent reviewers calibrated to that program's rubric score it blind; large disagreements go to a senior reviewer.

### Can strong answers make up for a bad one?

Rarely. Under summed scoring an extreme low score keeps dragging the total, so steady 4s beat an occasional 5.

### What separates a 3 from a 5?

A 3 is clear and relevant but shallow. A 5 has vivid real detail, composure under pressure and a clear account of how the experience changed what you do.

### How should I use two minutes of prep?

0:00–0:20 identify the competency and pick a story, 0:20–0:50 outline STAR, 0:50–1:10 write your reflection line, then rehearse silently and face the lens.
